# Data collection for the puck-pushing policy: analysis of 13 approaches, schedule and episode budget

Filed as `06_deliverables/research_notes/Recording tool/2026-10-30_data_collection_analysis.md`. Written 30 October 2026 for Emranul Hoque, Marshal Sebastine and Prof. Nierhoff (Team M, AI Project, Project 6, OTH Amberg-Weiden).

How to read the markings. "(measured)" is a number the team measured on the robot. "(calc)" is arithmetic on measured numbers. "(guess)" is an estimate with no measurement behind it. "(inference)" is our own reasoning that no cited source states. Every other number carries a link to the source that states it. A source marked "use with care" is real, but at least one number in circulation about it was wrong or needs the stated qualification; the correct numbers are used here and the wrong ones are named. A source marked "(unverified)" was not checked against the original in this pass and is used only for colour, never for a decision. Emranul and Marshal decide; everything below is a recommendation.

## 0. Summary in plain language

The dataset can be made useless by six things: training pictures that do not look like test pictures; labels that the two operators give differently for the same picture; pictures in which the line ahead is not visible; too few examples of the rare moves (left, right, back, stop); a puck that slides differently under a replay than under a hand; and unsafe hands on a torque-free arm. Every recommendation below guards against one of these, and every guard has a test in section 6.

1. Keep keyboard stepping as the main way to record. Its training pictures are exactly the pictures the robot will see at test time, it is the most consistent method, and it is the safest. Its weaknesses are in the tool and the protocol, not in the idea, and they are fixable this weekend.
2. Hand-guided "guide, then replay" stays as a measured comparison arm, not as an equal source. Change its 400 ms override window to "Enter accepts, a direction key overrides", measure it against keyboard on sheet 01 this weekend and on sheet 02 with bracket F, and keep it only if the replay rarely needs overrides. The default plan assumes it becomes a minority supplement or an on-screen suggestion while a person still presses every key.
3. Keep the seven classes. Add tool keys (a "previous step was a mistake" key and a "lost" key), not action classes, until the wish-list notes show a recurring situation that the camera can see.
4. Train the first model after 30 to 40 episodes, not after 100 to 150, and start human-gated correction rounds (model proposes, a person accepts or overrides every step) as soon as that model completes about half of sheet 01. Corrections are the best-evidenced fix for drift and cost the same robot minutes as plain episodes.
5. About 30 percent of keyboard episodes start with a deliberate puck or rod offset and about 25 percent start near the line end, so recovery and stop frames exist from day one. Random substituted steps (DART style, epsilon 0.05 to 0.10) come after the first model.
6. Real training data waits for bracket F (from 7 November). Everything recorded this weekend with the stock camera is rehearsal.
7. Robot time until 20 December is about 40 productive hours (calc, assumptions in section 5), of which 4 are reserved for fitting, direction tests and re-teaching. The recommended plan needs about 30 of the remaining 36 hours (calc with the traced sheet lengths; the meander alone is 77 cm), leaving a buffer of just under one weekend; without printed sheets about 27 hours.

## 1. What drives the choice here

- The camera window and the 2 November change. The stock wrist camera sees about 8.5 cm across at the puck and 10 cm at the top, from the puck's centre to about 7 cm ahead, with the puck always at the bottom of the picture (measured). The line ahead is visible only while the path heads within about 45 degrees of "away from the base" (calc). Sheets 03 to 06 therefore have long blind stretches with the stock camera. Bracket F (camera about 5 cm ahead of the rod axis and 6 cm above the wrist axis, looking nearly straight down; calc 89 to 98 percent of the hard sheets observable) arrives on Monday 2 November. Pictures are tied to the camera position, so no picture taken before the bracket is fitted may enter the training set, and stock-camera and bracket-F pictures must never be mixed in one training set (inference). Every pixel calibration done this weekend (reference column, pixels per centimetre, anchor) is void on Monday. Wrist-only policies work while the target stays in view: hand-camera policies train and generalise worse when the task-relevant target leaves the hand camera's view ([61](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2203.12677)).
- One decision per second. A 1 cm linear step costs 0.77 to 1.2 s at the 20 percent speed cap (measured). The camera delivers about 8.9 new JPEGs per second while reads return stale copies about 190 times per second (measured), so the tool waits for two new pictures after a move, 0.11 to 0.22 s (calc). A human decision costs 1 to 2 s (guess). Every pyniryo call blocks ([59](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/NiryoRobotics/pyniryo/master/pyniryo/api/tcp_client.py)). This is Ferrell's move-and-wait regime: completion time is predictable from the delay, the no-delay completion time and the number of open-loop moves, and no collection method shortens it ([57](https://dspace.mit.edu/handle/1721.1/11065)). Collection speed is set by the robot, not by the input device (inference), and the human has unlimited thinking time per step.
- Discrete seven-class actions. The policy outputs forward, forward_left, forward_right, left, right, back or stop in the picture's frame (decision of 28.10.26), executed as a 1 cm step. Discrete tokens with cross-entropy beat regression in RT-1's ablation (97 to 68 percent on seen tasks when tokens were replaced by continuous actions) ([52](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.06817)), so the class head is sound. The label is the action, so keyboard data has no quantisation error; any smooth path (hand or gamepad) must be snapped onto 45 degree classes, which creates label flicker near class boundaries (inference). Discrete classes also make label agreement measurable and make class imbalance a first-class risk.
- Two beginner operators on two laptops. Operator consistency is the best-documented cause of degradation in small imitation datasets. On robomimic Square, behaviour cloning scored 58 percent with 200 demonstrations from one proficient operator and 36, 12 and 2 percent with 100 demonstrations from the Better, Okay and Worse groups; the authors recommend curating toward more consistent actions ([2](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2306.02437), use with care). Image-based behaviour cloning fell from 62.0 to 46.0 percent on Square and from 55.3 to 18.7 percent on Transport when 300 demonstrations from six operators replaced 200 from one ([4](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2108.03298)).
- Seven weeks of robot time, mostly weekends. Seven full weekends lie between 7 November and 20 December. Section 5 turns this into about 40 productive hours with explicit assumptions.
- The grading rule. Finishing the track comes first, then finishing time against other groups, on the six provided sheets and on unseen sheets of the same size and line width. A slow model that finishes beats a fast one that fails; zig-zag and oscillation cost time but not the grade; any drift off the line costs the grade. Generalisation to unseen sheets probably follows the number of distinct line shapes in training rather than repeats of one shape (inference from [15](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.18647), which measures environments and objects, not line shapes).
- "Imitation learning only, we push the puck ourselves." The professor allowed keyboard driving and hand-guiding and pretrained vision models (26.10.26). Any step executed or label produced by code (a path follower, a colour detector, a geometric teacher) is outside those words unless he approves it (inference). This single constraint removes three of the thirteen approaches as label sources.

## 2. Every approach

The verdicts below are ranked by what verified studies show. Where the literature is silent, this is said.

### 2.1 keyboard_stepping (keyboard stepping: one key per 1 cm step from the camera picture)

How it works here. One person at the laptop sees only the recording window; the second person stands at the robot with the Stop button. Per step, `__run_steps` in `02_code/record_episodes.py` waits for the blocking move, waits for two new JPEGs (`steps_until_fresh_picture = 2`), reads pose and joints, shows the picture, blocks on a key (q w e / a s d, Space, x), writes the raw JPEG bytes and a `steps.csv` row (step, unix_time, frame, md5, action, source, pose, joints), converts the picture-frame action to a table displacement with `config.picture_step_to_table`, checks the workspace box and moves 1 cm.

Strengths. Training pictures are literally the test pictures: same code path, same freshness guard, static arm, no hand, no blur, no timing mismatch. UMI measured the cost of a timing mismatch between observation and action at 30 points (105 of 120 with latency matching against 69 of 120 without) ([11](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.10329v3)); this loop avoids the problem by construction. The label is the policy's own action space. Consistency is cheap because the operator has unlimited thinking time per step, and consistency is what the small-data evidence rewards ([2](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2306.02437), use with care). Nobody touches the arm. The same loop later hosts the model as decider. Episodes are short and pause-free by construction, which is what practitioner guidance asks for ([24](https://isaac-sim.github.io/IsaacLab/v2.0.2/source/overview/teleop_imitation.html)). The usual keyboard penalty (the slowest device in RoboTurk, 151 s against 79 s for VR on a picking task ([21](https://arxiv.org/abs/1811.02790)); "SpaceMouse is better than Keyboard" ([24](https://isaac-sim.github.io/IsaacLab/v2.0.2/source/overview/teleop_imitation.html))) is about smoothness in continuous control and does not apply when the robot caps the rate at one step per second and axis-aligned steps are the intended action space (inference). A keyboard is a standard supported demonstration device in robosuite ([75](https://robosuite.ai/docs/algorithms/demonstrations.html)) and LeRobot ([7](https://huggingface.co/docs/lerobot/en/il_robots)).

Weaknesses and severity.
- Major: operator-versus-model information gap. The person decides from 640x480 with memory of earlier frames and, unless prevented, a glance at the real sheet; the model decides from one downscaled frame. An expert with more information than the learner is "impossibly good" and no learner can reproduce it ([53](https://iclr.cc/virtual/2023/poster/11786)); off-policy imitation of such an expert latches onto repeating past actions ([25](https://arxiv.org/abs/2208.02225)). Vision in Action restricts the human to the robot's own view for exactly this reason ([26](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.15666v1)).
- Major: label inconsistency at class boundaries between the two operators (when is the line "off enough" for a diagonal; when to press stop). The stepping rule is unwritten. See 2.12.
- Major: class imbalance built in. On sheet 01 an episode is about 24 forward, 2 to 6 diagonals and 1 stop (calc); left, right and back appear only after mistakes. Equal weighting of an imbalanced demonstration set provably gives an imbalanced policy ([56](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.06319)).
- Major: queued and slipped keys. `cv2.waitKey` buffers presses made during the move, so a held or double-pressed key labels a picture the operator never saw (inference from the code; the key reader never drains the buffer). The only remedy today is discarding the whole episode with `x`, which costs 1.5 to 3 minutes (calc).
- Fatal for the stock camera on sheets 03 to 06 only: blind stretches cannot be labelled from the picture; such frames are learnt as noise or as "repeat the last step", the copycat shortcut ([54](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.14876)). The plan already records no real data there. Not fatal with bracket F if blind frames stay under about 10 percent per sheet (guess).
- Major: multi-step recovery plans live in the operator's head; a single-picture model cannot carry them.
- Major: metadata gaps. `episode.json` records no camera mount, sheet orientation or start index, deliberate start offset, lighting or mapping version; session folders are named by date and episode numbers count per laptop, so two laptops recording on one day collide at merge.
- Major: the picture-frame to table mapping (`camera_heading_rad`, `picture_left_sign`) is part of the dataset's definition; a silent config change between sessions would poison the dataset with no visible error.
- Minor: coarse 45 degree classes force zig-zag on a line that runs about 9 degrees off picture-forward at the sheet 01 start (calc from the taught coordinates, about 2.4 cm over 15.6 cm); more steps cost time, not the grade.
- Minor: the workspace guard checks the rod, not the puck. The box's far edge is the table edge (`box_x` max 0.450 m) while the puck centre sits 1.85 cm beyond the tip (tip radius 0.65 cm plus puck radius 1.2 cm, calc), so forward keys past the line end can push the puck off the table.
- Minor: fatigue over about 13,000 keyboard key decisions plus about 6,000 gated decisions in the recommended plan, about 9,500 per operator if shared equally (calc); no verified source on its size.

Evidence and what the literature says. In a modality comparison on real manipulation, kinesthetic data won on two of three tasks because it was clean and consistent (low action variance), and replay was needed to recover actions and to get pictures "unimpeded by the demonstrator's hand" ([1](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.07017v1)); keyboard stepping gets both without a replay (inference). Causal confusion says more inputs can make imitation worse ([55](https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.11979)), so pose and joints stay out of the model. No paper compares keyboard stepping with any other modality for a discrete classification policy at about 1 Hz; every interface comparison found is about continuous control. The verdict therefore rests on the consistency and timing-match evidence plus inference.

Verdict: primary.

What to correct in our plan or tool.
- Tool, before any real episode: after showing the fresh picture, drain pending keys (`waitKey(1)` until it returns -1) and only then accept input; log the time from picture shown to key and flag anything under 0.3 s (guess) as pre-pressed.
- Tool: add a key (`u`) that marks the previous row `is_mistake = 1` and keeps the episode; training drops flagged rows and the steps that undo them. Keep `x` for the whole episode.
- Tool: log move-return time, capture time and decision time separately (`steps.csv` has one `unix_time`, written after the decision).
- Tool, `episode.json`: add `camera_mount` (stock_wrist or bracket_F), a mapping version id for `picture_step_to_table` and `camera_heading_rad`, sheet orientation and start index, deliberate start offset (lateral cm, angle deg), lighting note, laptop. Refuse to train on mixed `camera_mount` or mapping ids.
- Tool: put the operator or laptop into the session folder name so two laptops cannot collide.
- Tool: extend the workspace guard to the predicted puck centre (rod target plus 1.85 cm along the step) against the sheet corners plus 2 cm.
- Display: draw a reference band on the shown picture only, never on the saved JPEG (stock camera: columns 150 to 200 when pushing straight, measured; re-measure for bracket F), and write the stepping rule in those pixel terms with hysteresis.
- Protocol: the keyboard operator looks only at the screen; the second person watches the robot. Show the operator the model-resolution picture (downscale and upscale for display) or pass the resolution agreement test in section 6. Quotas per sheet: 25 to 35 percent of episodes start with the puck 0.5 to 1 cm beside the line or the rod 15 to 30 degrees off the tangent; 20 to 30 percent start 5 to 8 cm before the line end so stop frames are not one per full episode only; several start points and both orientations where the camera allows.
- Deployment: the trained model must run as a decider inside the same `EpisodeRecorder` loop, with the same `__fresh_picture` and mapping functions. A separate deployment script with its own capture code breaks the match that is this approach's whole point.
- SOTA report section 2: "only because the recorded poses were replayed" overstates [1]; the paper gives clean, consistent data as the reason and replay as the way to obtain actions. The keyboard conclusion stands, for the consistency reason. Add the operator-versus-model information gap.

### 2.2 guide_then_replay (hand-guided: guide the puck with motors off, then the robot replays the tip path in 1 cm steps)

How it works here. `__record_guided_path` calls `set_learning_mode(True)`; Niryo's own status table defines learning mode as "Free motion enabled, the torques are disabled" ([58](https://github.com/NiryoRobotics/ned_ros/blob/master/docs/packages/high_level/niryo_robot_status.rst)). A person pushes the puck while the rod end's x, y, z are logged every 2 mm (`guided_path_sample_m`); orientation is not logged and no picture is kept. After Space the motors come back on, the puck is put back, and `__make_replay_decider` projects the current tip position onto the path, takes the point 1 cm ahead, proposes the moving class whose 1 cm step lands nearest to it (back included), shows it for `replay_key_wait_ms = 400` ms and executes it unless a key arrives. Only the replay is training data.

Strengths. Replay pictures are deployment pictures (static arm, no hand, same loop). Replay of recorded poses is the published way to get hand-free pictures from kinesthetic data ([1](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.07017v1); [16](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.21628), use with care; [35](https://arxiv.org/html/2311.01248)). The label rule is one deterministic function of the hand path, so the two operators cannot diverge on replay steps (inference). The per-step human load is a veto instead of a seven-way choice. The replay decider is structurally the correction-round decider, so the override, discard and `source` machinery is a dress rehearsal. Hand-guiding is fast per demonstration: 17 to 24 s kinesthetic against 26 to 65 s for a joystick in an eight-novice study ([37](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.28033)); 19.3 s per trajectory demonstration with 34 novices ([30](https://homes.cs.washington.edu/~mcakmak/pdfs/2012/akgun2012hri.pdf)). At about 1 cm per second a 9 g puck push is quasi-static, so stopping and starting along the same path should not by itself change the outcome (inference; 20 mm/s is treated as quasi-static and friction showed little speed dependence on three of four surfaces in [5](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1604.04038)). `guided_path.csv` is a free reference path.

Weaknesses and severity.
- Major: the replay is open loop on the puck. Labels come from the rod's place on the old path, not from what the picture shows. Even an identical 15 cm push, repeated 2,000 times, scattered by 3.4 to 11.7 mm and 1.3 to 4.5 degrees depending on the surface, with a multimodal distribution ([5](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1604.04038)). A point pusher on a disc has no stable-pushing condition; that theory is for edge pushers ([62](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1905.05138); [68](https://www.ri.cmu.edu/?p=14270), metadata only). In the modality study the contact task is where kinesthetic replay "falls short": the first replay did not succeed, the correction heuristic doubled replay time and made the actions highly jerky, and kinesthetic scored 0.5 against 0.95 and 0.85 for the two teleoperation modes on that task ([1](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.07017v1)). Ablett et al. name the same mechanism: a position replay may not reproduce the compliant motion made while the human pushed the arm ([35](https://arxiv.org/html/2311.01248)). When the puck drifts, the picture shows an off-centre puck while the label says "follow the old path": a systematic wrong label exactly where the right one is needed most (inference).
- Major: the 400 ms override window is shorter than a considered seven-way choice. Simple visual reaction time is about 190 ms and choice reaction time is longer ([60](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mental_chronometry), secondary); choice time grows with the logarithm of the number of alternatives ([78](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hick%27s_law), secondary, use with care). The override labels, the only puck-aware labels in replay data, land one step late or do not happen, and the mode silently becomes pure open-loop replay.
- Major: lookahead equals step length, the "oscillation prone" corner of pure pursuit ([63](https://av2.readthedocs.io/en/latest/theory/pure-pursuit.html), secondary). Near 22.5 degree headings the proposal flips between forward and a diagonal on near-identical pictures. Hand tremor and joint-sensor noise add velocity spikes, which is why kinesthetic paths are smoothed before use ([64](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.09802)).
- Major: only x, y are replayed; roll, pitch and yaw are not logged. A 10 degree rod tilt during guiding displaces the magnet tip (2.6 cm below the rod end) by 4.5 mm, 20 degrees by 9 mm (calc), so the replayed contact path can sit beside the hand's contact path.
- Major: torque-off sag and unused z. The arm sags into the hand when torques drop and those first points are logged; segments where the tip was lifted and the puck did not move become confident forward labels.
- Major: no representation of lifts or blind stretches; hairpins need the pusher to leave contact, and an x, y replay ploughs into the puck.
- Major: torque handling is not exception-safe. Ctrl-C during guiding reaches `record_session`'s `finally`, which calls `move_home` while a person holds a torque-free arm (confirmed in the code; what `move_home` does in learning mode is unchecked, `robot_link.py` not read).
- Major: mirrored labels are silent. The replay chooses and executes with the same `picture_step_to_table`, so a wrong `picture_left_sign` tracks the path correctly while every stored left and right label is mirrored. Keyboard mode reveals the sign; replay data hides it.
- Minor: "back" is in `moving_actions`, so the snapping rule can propose a backward step the hand never made. Minor: after a detected collision the ROS wrapper refuses further moves until the flag is cleared ([72](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/NiryoRobotics/ned_ros/master/niryo_robot_python_ros_wrapper/src/niryo_robot_python_ros_wrapper/ros_wrapper.py)), and the `finally` then sends home from 5 mm above the paper. Minor: time, about 1.5 times a keyboard episode with the confirm variant (calc, section 5). Minor: puck reset by eye (plus or minus 2 to 3 mm, guess).

Evidence and what the literature says. Mixing a small kinesthetic set with teleoperation data beat the best single modality by about 5 percent ([1](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.07017v1)); the "about 20 percent on average" figure in earlier drafts misrepresents the paper and is not used. Kinesthetic data suffer from the human "or even the shadow" in the camera frame, which is why Ablett et al. reset and replay ([35](https://arxiv.org/html/2311.01248)). Novice kinesthetic trajectories are noisy ([30](https://homes.cs.washington.edu/~mcakmak/pdfs/2012/akgun2012hri.pdf)). No paper replays a hand-guided path as blocking 1 cm steps, and none reports how far a replayed pushing trajectory deviates from the hand's puck path. The pushing-physics papers say the deviation cannot be zero. The modality study [1] says the pushing task is where replay broke.

Verdict: secondary (measured pilot; keep only if the numbers in section 6 pass).

What to correct.
- Replace the 400 ms timeout with confirm-by-default for the first sessions: Enter executes the proposal, a direction key overrides, Space stops, `x` discards, no timeout. Flush keys after the picture is shown; log whether the key arrived before or after the picture. If a timeout is kept later, it must be at least 1.5 s.
- Add a lookahead parameter of 2 to 3 cm separate from `step_m`; smooth the guided path with a moving average over about 2 cm; add hysteresis (keep the previous action when within 1 mm of the best); exclude "back" from proposals.
- Log roll, pitch, yaw and the six joints in `guided_path.csv`; compute the contact-tip path (rod end plus 2.6 cm along the rod axis) and replay that path shifted to the rod end for a vertical rod; start logging only after a "go" key once the tip is settled behind the puck; truncate at the first sample more than 10 mm above `pushing_height`; discard episodes with z outside plus or minus 5 mm for more than 10 percent of samples; write these QA numbers into `episode.json`.
- Make the guiding phase exception-safe: on any exception leave learning mode on, print "let go of the arm, then press Enter", re-enable torque only then, lift before any home move; show the torque state read back from `get_learning_mode()` in large letters.
- Gate the mode on the direction test: refuse `--mode hand_guided` until `picture_left_sign` is marked tested in `config.py`.
- Make the replay decider implement the same deadband and stop rule as the written keyboard rule, or keep replay data as a separately flagged `source`.
- Checklist step 5 (one hand-guided episode) is too thin for today's keep-or-drop decision; run the measurements of section 6.
- SOTA report section 2: "collapses into keyboard stepping plus extra time" misses both the gain (one deterministic label rule) and the main risk (open-loop labels that can contradict the picture). It also misreads [1]: the doubling was of the replay time on the contact task, not of collection time, and the pushing task is where kinesthetic was worst.

### 2.3 raw_kinesthetic (use the pictures taken while the hand pushes)

How it works here. During the guide phase the tool already reads a picture every poll (inside `__show_picture`) and discards it. The variant saves every new JPEG with its arrival time and the nearest pose (about 3 to 4 frames per centimetre at 3 cm/s, calc), cuts the path into 1 cm chords and bins the chord heading into the seven classes.

Strengths. Cheapest robot time by far: a 24 cm hand push is 10 to 20 s (guess), zero marginal cost as a by-product of the guide phase. The hand-in-view objection is weaker than the SOTA report states: the picture starts at the puck's centre and looks away from the base (measured), so a forearm or elbow grip is behind the lens (inference, untested). Motion blur is probably small: at 3 cm/s and a 1/30 s exposure the scene moves about 1 mm, 7 px against a 75 px line (calc; the exposure is unknown). Natural pushing contains small re-centring motions the grid cannot express.

Weaknesses and severity. Five independent mismatches between raw pictures and test pictures, each major on its own.
- Rod tilt becomes camera tilt. The camera follows the rod's lean (measured); with motors off the rod angle is whatever the hands hold; at test time the rod is vertical. Single perturbation factors such as camera pose cut manipulation success by 30 to 50 percent in a general benchmark ([65](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.08191)).
- Tip height drifts. The lens is about 8 cm above the paper (guess; not traceable to a logged measurement), so 1 cm of height rescales the picture by about 12 percent (calc on that guess).
- Picture-to-pose timing is unknown. At 3 cm/s a 200 ms latency misplaces a frame by 6 mm (calc); UMI's 30-point latency result applies ([11](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.10329v3)).
- The guiding person steers from a privileged view of the whole sheet ([25](https://arxiv.org/abs/2208.02225); [61](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2203.12677)).
- A smooth path binned at 45 degrees flips near boundaries, and label noise hurts most with few episodes (PMObstacle at 10 episodes: 78.0 with action-label noise against 90.3 with state noise) ([2](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2306.02437), use with care).
- Also major: operator shadow across the sheet counts as human presence ([35](https://arxiv.org/html/2311.01248)); lighting is among the most damaging perturbations ([65](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.08191)). Also major: safety. Free motion means no torque, the Ned2 has no brakes ([70](https://docs.niryo.com/product/niryo-studio/source/specification.html), use with care), the arm can drop when power is cut or the emergency stop is pressed ([71](https://archive-docs.niryo.com/product/ned2/v1.0.0/en/source/hardware/safety_instructions.html), use with care, page unreachable), the FreeMotion button releases torque while held ([92](https://niryo.com/ned2/)), and the natural place to steer the rod is the wrist link, where the camera and bracket sit.
- Minor: motion blur is a known corruption that raises CNN error sharply ([67](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1903.12261)); a continuous push under-represents post-step states; 90 correlated frames per episode are not 90 samples.

Evidence and what the literature says. Every group that trains a vision policy from hand-guided data removes the human first. Training on raw kinesthetic pictures with the operator in view gave 0 of 20 on all nine KineDex tasks until the body was inpainted out; kinesthetic took half to under a third of the teleoperation time on the two tasks where timing was compared ([31](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.01974v1)). DexUMI's ablation: raw frames 0.20 and 0.05 success, masked 0.60 and 0.10, inpainted 0.95 and 0.90 ([49](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.21864)). Kinesthetic demonstrations are "inappropriate due to the undesirable appearance of human arms" ([10](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2008.04899); [9](https://arxiv.org/abs/1710.04615)) and suffer "visual distractions" from the human's intervention ([66](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.09574)). No source measures how often a guiding hand enters a wrist camera's view, nor the effect of a shadow alone, nor blur. Our geometry differs, so the tests in section 6 decide.

Verdict: secondary (log for free; train on it only after a closed-loop A/B test).

What to correct. Save guide-phase frames to `<episode>/guided_frames/` with a `guided_frames.csv` (time, md5, pose, roll, pitch, yaw, joints, `source = guided_raw`), never into `frames/` or `steps.csv`. Add a one-off camera-latency measurement to the first bracket-F session (scripted constant-speed move over the line with frames and poses logged, UMI's method) and store it in `config.py` as measured. Two-person grip rule: one carries the elbow, one steers a marked zone on the forearm below the wrist link, never the wrist link, camera or bracket; checklist step 5's "hold the arm at the elbow" is not enough once the wrist joints go slack. SOTA report section 2 states as fact that hand-guiding "puts your hand into the camera view and gives blurred frames"; for our geometry both are untested and the blur is probably a few pixels (calc); replace with the measurement.

### 2.4 trace_line_then_replay (trace the empty line by hand once, then replay it with the puck)

How it works here. With motors off a person moves the tip along the printed line with no puck; the tool logs x, y; then the puck is placed and the replay decider snaps 1 cm steps onto the trace, with the override window as in 2.2, for every episode on that sheet.

Strengths. Fastest label production on paper (one trace per sheet, about 1 to 2 minutes, guess; a 24 cm replay about 30 s at 1.2 s per step, calc). Test-like pictures. Low human load. Useful by-products regardless: the line in robot coordinates for the observable-fraction calculation, for metrics and for a post-hoc audit of human labels.

Weaknesses and severity.
- Fatal: the labels are not a function of the picture and ignore the puck. After drift the picture shows an off-centre puck and the label says "follow the line". A teacher whose states collapse into one student observation yields conflicting labels and an "increasingly unrealizable" dataset ([33](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.09546v1), use with care; [34](https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.12173)). For a classifier whose only varying cue is the puck's offset from the line, a dataset of "line centred means forward" teaches nothing about recovery (inference).
- Fatal: an open-loop point push of a disc is laterally unstable. With 1.85 cm between tip and puck centres, a tip 2 mm off the puck's axis pushes about 6 degrees sideways, 5 mm about 16 degrees (calc, frictionless point contact). Pushing is "sensitive to initial conditions" and pushes indistinguishable at sensor resolution give variable results ([62](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1905.05138)). Open-loop replay of transformed demonstration segments in MimicGen succeeded in 20.7 to 100 percent of attempts depending on the task, and 5 mm plus 5 degree object-pose noise dropped generation success from 73.7 to 60.9 percent ([32](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2310.17596), use with care: these are per-task data-generation success rates for transformed segments, partially verified); hand-placing the puck is such noise.
- Major: puck re-placement error by hand (2 to 3 mm, guess) feeds that instability.
- Major: the trace is the wrong path. The puck's centre sits 1.85 cm ahead of the tip, so the stop rule (remaining trace under half a step) drives the tip to the line end and the puck about 1.85 cm past it (calc), beyond the 1.2 cm goal tolerance; on curves the tip path should be offset inward.
- Major: the override gate, as in 2.2. Major: no on-policy benefit; the states are a path follower's, not the learner's, and a policy must perform well under the state distribution it induces ([13](https://arxiv.org/abs/1011.0686)). Major: rules, since nobody pushes the puck during the trace and the label maker is a path follower.
- Minor: hand-trace jitter; no puck measurement in the logs; the time saving is small because a human still sits at every replay.

Evidence. As above, plus the contact-task replay failure in [1](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.07017v1) and the pushing scatter in [5](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1604.04038).

Verdict: drop as a label source. Keep two by-products: the trace, or better the registered SVG, as a geometry reference, and the override code as the skeleton of the correction-round decider.

What to correct. Do not adopt. If any path ever describes the puck's centre line, the stop test in `__make_replay_decider` must become `< contact_offset + step_m / 2` with `contact_offset = 0.0185 m`, and the target must be the path point minus that offset along the tangent. Register the SVG to the taught sheet corners (homography) for the observable-fraction computation, the lateral-deviation metric and a post-hoc audit of human labels. Add an overhead phone photo at the start and end of every episode (sheet corners in view) for scoring only; without it "keep imperfect successes, discard failures" has no measurement behind it. Training filter: train only on `source` human steps or policy steps a human accepted by an explicit key; treat `replay` steps as unlabelled pictures unless the pilot in 2.2 passes.

### 2.5 scripted_privileged_teacher (a script that knows the line in table coordinates labels frames)

How it works here. The line polyline per sheet in table coordinates (sheet 01 is taught; others from the SVG through the four taught corners, or 20 to 40 tip touches per sheet, 15 to 30 minutes, guess). Variant A: the script drives and labels (the demonstration source). Variant B: the script only verifies human labels offline (agreement, lateral sign, progress, stop correctness). Variant C: the script labels states the trained model visits (DAgger with a geometric oracle).

Strengths. Perfectly consistent labels. It is the textbook DAgger oracle. On a real rally car, batch imitation reached completion figures of 51, 53 and 69 percent at 6,000, 9,000 and 12,000 samples while online labels from a privileged MPC expert gave 100 percent at every size ([39](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1709.07174), use with care: the paper's metric is not literally "percent of runs"). A privileged planner labelled a depth-only student's visited states entirely in simulation and the real quadrotor flew without a crash at 3 and 5 m/s ([73](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2110.05113)). Direct imitation of an autopilot gave 20 percent, two-stage training through a privileged agent 100 percent, in simulation ([38](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1912.12294)). Privileged supervision computed offline on logged frames gave a 25 percent higher driving score with 40 times less data, also in simulation ([74](https://arxiv.org/abs/2105.00636)), which supports variants B and C in principle. Variant B gives the section-4 metrics and a check of `picture_left_sign` and `camera_heading_rad` from the first three keyboard episodes with zero robot time.

Weaknesses and severity.
- Fatal (variant A): a model trained on frames labelled by a line-following script is a learnt copy of a programmed line follower; the course constraint and the professor's words exclude it without his written agreement.
- Major: the teacher knows the line but not the puck, so it labels "follow the line" while the puck slides off an off-centre contact ([5](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1604.04038)).
- Major: unrealisable labels on blind stretches. Three privileged teachers with identical rewards, architecture and hyperparameters all exceeded 98 percent, but their egocentric students reached 0, 54.8 and 87.3 percent; the only difference was the teacher's observation function ([69](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.18071)). Querying a privileged teacher at states the student cannot distinguish produces conflicting labels ([33](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.09546v1), use with care).
- Major: two stepping rules in one dataset if script labels and human labels are mixed, the multi-operator problem in software form; label flicker at class boundaries without lookahead and hysteresis.
- Minor: registration errors (1 to 2 mm touch error, guess); no hairpin manoeuvre in a line-only teacher; verifier over-trust; autonomous motion during collection. More scripted data is not better data: behaviour cloning from a privileged autopilot showed "performance degrading as more data is collected" and was best at 10 of 100 hours ([14](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1904.08980)); robomimic's machine-generated sets gave 65.3 and 64.7 percent against 100 and 95.3 from proficient human data ([4](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2108.03298)).

Evidence and what the literature says. Lane-following imitation with a scripted pure-pursuit expert needed 98,304 behaviour-cloning samples plus 65,536 DAgger samples, and DAgger then had 0 major infractions against 0.23 to 0.63 ([12](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2206.10797)). Privileged-teacher labelling of visited states has the strongest evidence of any method for fixing rare states, but all hardware results are vehicles with state sensors, and no real-robot pushing study with a scripted teacher was found.

Verdict: secondary, split by variant. B (offline verifier, metrics, no labels): adopt now. C: only with the professor's written approval, puck-aware and visibility-restricted, stored under `source = script` and compared with and without on the robot. A: do not use.

What to correct. Do not add a scripted mode to `record_episodes.py`. Add a separate read-only `02_code/verify_episode_labels.py` that computes per step the nearest line point, arc progress, tip lateral offset, the geometric ideal class with 2 to 3 cm lookahead and hysteresis, agreement with the logged action, and stop correctness. Add per-sheet line polylines (or the SVG plus corner homography) to `config.py`. Checklist (section 6): run the verifier on the three keyboard episodes; lateral-sign agreement below 50 percent means `picture_left_sign` or `camera_heading_rad` is wrong. SOTA report section 2 paraphrases [33] imprecisely: the hazard is conflicting labels at the same student observation, not querying "at states far from anything realisable". Add that Learning by Cheating's privileged agent was itself cloned from an autopilot and that both stages ran in simulation.

### 2.6 human_gated_corrections (the trained model proposes each step, a person accepts or overrides)

How it works here. A `policy_gated` mode reusing the step loop and the replay decider's structure with the classifier as proposer: the stored picture goes through the model (tens of milliseconds, inference), the window shows the proposal and its top-2 probabilities, the operator presses an explicit accept key (Enter, never Space, never a timeout) or a direction key; the executed step is always human-endorsed; `steps.csv` logs proposal, probabilities, decision time and `source` in {policy_accepted, human_override, human_blind}.

Strengths. It is the only collection-method class with repeated real-robot evidence that it removes compounding drift. AutoRally: 51 to 69 percent batch against 100 percent online ([39](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1709.07174), use with care). HG-DAgger on a real vehicle: 0 collisions and 0 road departures in the first 5,000 samples against 1 collision and 6 road departures for behaviour cloning (Table II) ([8](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1810.02890), use with care: the figures 11 and 66 in our SOTA report are wrong). IWR from a 30-demonstration seed: Threading 87.3 percent against 76.7 for an equal number of extra demonstrations, Coffee 87.5 against 64.9 ([18](https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.06733), use with care). Sirius: plus 8 percent in simulation and plus 27 percent on real hardware ([17](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.08416)). RaC: 78.3 percent with 5 hours of data where about 89 hours (5,345 demonstrations) of plain demonstrations reached 75.0 ([19](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.07953)). Our blocking loop removes the two things that degraded human labels in those studies: reaction lag (Ross et al. labelled a flying learner's frames live with a joystick, which introduced reaction delays ([50](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1211.1690), use with care)) and control handover during motion, HG-DAgger's own argument for gating ([8](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1810.02890)). The override is executed and its effect is seen in the next picture, which removes the "without observing the effect" problem of retroactive robot-centric labels (about 40 against 60 percent success) ([22](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1610.00850)). Discrete classification is DAgger's original setting ([13](https://arxiv.org/abs/1011.0686)). Pictures are test pictures. Nobody touches the arm. Every step is a free diagnostic.

Weaknesses and severity.
- Major: rounds of 20 to 40 episodes are thinner than any study that reports gains. IWR and Sirius ran each round until intervention samples reached one third of the initial dataset ([18](https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.06733); [17](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.08416)); HG-DAgger added five rounds of 2,000 labels to a 10,000 seed ([8](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1810.02890)).
- Major: the override rule is unspecified. Override every disagreement and no drift state is visited; override late and wrong steps become labels.
- Major: anchoring. Automation bias with imperfect decision aids produces omission and commission errors, occurs in novices and experts alike and is not removed by brief practice ([36](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/0018720810376055)). Accepted frames become labels.
- Major: imitation gap at states where the puck or line has left the window; the human sees the table, the model does not.
- Major: dependence on the base model and the hardware state; any re-mount, focus-ring touch or lamp change between base data and rounds voids both.
- Major: tool gaps. `__run_steps` re-asks the decider after a workspace refusal with `continue`; with a deterministic proposer and an unchanged scene it stalls forever. `x` discards a whole episode, throwing away the labels the method exists to collect. No proposal or probability logging.
- Minor: progress between rounds may not be monotonic (inference; not supported by the verified content of [50]); corrections fix drift on the sheets used, not generalisation to unseen shapes ([15](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.18647)); rare override classes; safety of a chain of accepted non-forward steps; disclosure to the professor.

Evidence and what the literature says. Keeping the robot's accepted samples with balanced batches beat interventions-only (HG-DAgger style) by 12 to 18 points: 87.3 and 87.5 against 75.3 and 69.6 ([18](https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.06733), use with care). Sirius weights intervention samples to one half of every batch and zeroes the about 15 robot actions (about 2 s of human reaction time) just before an intervention ([17](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.08416); our evidence pack's "22 s" was a transcription error). Non-intervention "provides information" about the state and the action ([40](https://www.roboticsproceedings.org/rss16/p055.html)). LeRobot's human-in-the-loop page on the main branch records only the human correction windows by default and fine-tunes on them; the stable page differs in details, so which segments are kept depends on the version ([6](https://huggingface.co/docs/lerobot/hil_data_collection), use with care); it intervenes on an imminent "irreversible mistake", hesitation or deviation. No interactive imitation study uses a discrete step at about 1 Hz with a blocking move, and none measures whether rounds improve generalisation to unseen environments.

Verdict: primary (second stage; it cannot replace the initial keyboard demonstrations).

What to correct.
- SOTA report section 2, "train on demonstrations plus human-labelled corrections only": change to keep every executed step with its `source`, train with balanced batches (half human-labelled, half accepted), weight the one or two accepted steps before an override at zero.
- Round size by frames, not episodes: continue until at least 300 override frames exist (about 25 to 45 episodes at a 15 to 25 percent override rate and 47 to 50 steps per episode, about 40 to 60 episodes at 10 to 15 percent, calc) or the override rate on the round's sheets falls below 5 percent. Two such rounds fit the 22 November to 6 December window.
- Write the override rule beside the stepping rule: accept while the puck centre stays within the reference band (about 1 cm) and the rod keeps contact; override a proposal that would leave the band, lose contact, stop before the end marker or fail to stop at it.
- Start criterion: bracket F fitted, at least 30 to 40 keyboard episodes, base model completes at least 5 of 10 closed-loop trials on sheet 01 and 3 of 10 on sheet 02 (inference).
- Tool: `policy_gated` decider with explicit accept and no timeout; on a refused proposal fall back to the keyboard decider for that step; replace episode discard with `u` (previous step bad) and `l` (lost: end as failed, keep earlier steps); add columns `proposal`, `probabilities`, `decision_time_s`; soft limit of three consecutive non-forward accepted steps.
- Blind-first sub-mode: every fifth gated step hides the proposal until the human has typed; if the accept rate on shown steps exceeds the agreement rate on blind steps by more than 15 points (inference), run the rest of the round blind-first.
- Do not apply "discard failures" to gated episodes; keep all human-labelled steps up to the failure.
- Every round includes at least two freshly printed paths if allowed, else new orientations and starts; measure every round with 10 closed-loop trials on two seen sheets before and after, in one session.
- Tell the professor in one paragraph before the first round: the robot executes model-proposed steps that a student approves one by one; every label is human-chosen or human-approved; no reward, no reinforcement learning.

### 2.7 perturbation_recovery (DART-style noise: deliberate offsets and random substituted steps)

How it works here. (A) Epsilon-greedy substitution: after the operator presses a move key, the tool with probability epsilon executes a different 1 cm step; the frame keeps the pressed key as its label (DART's label is the supervisor's clean control at the visited state), and the next frame gets the operator's recovery key, chosen from the picture. (B) Physical offsets: start episodes with the puck 0.5 to 1 cm beside the line or the rod 15 to 30 degrees off the tangent, including mid-path starts before curves; needs no code.

Strengths. Recovery states before any model exists, off-policy, no retraining in the loop. Real-robot effects are large. DART on grasping in clutter with a Toyota HSR, 40 demonstrations per supervisor (10 clean plus 30 noisy): 79 percent against 49 for behaviour cloning, 72 percent when the noise was doubled, which the authors judged potentially too high for the human ([27](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v78/laskey17a.html)); DART's loss covers discrete action sets and its discrete noise is epsilon-greedy ([27](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/1703.09327), use with care). Injected steering noise in only about 10 percent of the simulated driving data, with the driver's corrective response as the only label, raised success from 56 to 88 percent (Town 1) and 22 to 64 percent (Town 2), and on a physical 1/5-scale truck missed intersections fell from 24.4 to 0 percent ([41](https://arxiv.org/abs/1710.02410)). System (transition) noise during collection raised robomimic Square from 40.0 to 69.7 percent with 50 episodes and from 55.7 to 82.0 with 200, while noise on the labels hurt ([2](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2306.02437), use with care: the 200-episode value at sigma 0.3 is 50.7, not 54.3 as in our evidence pack). Synthesised recovery views lifted eye-in-hand pushing from 20 to 80 percent with 8 demonstrations ([3](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.17768), use with care). Labels stay human and picture-grounded. States stay near the operator's own trajectory, the human-centric case ([22](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1610.00850)). The discrete blocking interface makes a perturbation exactly one class step. Physical offsets are plainly "we choose the start".

Weaknesses and severity.
- Major: recovery labels will be the least consistent labels in the dataset; two operators disagree most on states they rarely see. Retroactive labels correlated only 0.22 to 0.60 with real controls in a 10-person study ([22](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1610.00850)); our perturbed frames are closer to the human-centric case because the operator sees the effect, but the rule must be written and tested.
- Major: the noise level is a guess; the SOTA report's 0.15 to 0.25 is probably too high. DART sizes its noise from the learner's own per-step disagreement rate ([27](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v78/laskey17a.html)); for a first model with 5 to 10 percent held-out error that is epsilon 0.05 to 0.10 (inference).
- Major: puck physics after a wrong step is not "press the inverse key". From a directly-behind contact a pure lateral 1 cm tip step moves the tip away from the puck (centre distance grows from 1.85 to about 2.10 cm, calc), so it probably does not move the puck at all; whether the magnet block's body touches the puck is a measurement item. A forward push with the tip 0.5 to 1.0 cm off-centre acts well off the puck's axis and drives it sideways by several millimetres per centimetre (calc, frictionless point contact).
- Major: uniform random substitutions may not produce the states the deployed classifier reaches (late turns at curves rather than lateral offsets on straights); substitutes drawn from the confusion matrix after the first model are better (inference).
- Minor: the current logging cannot represent a substitution (one `action` column, `source` in {human, replay}); in replay mode a perturbation would be "recovered" by path snapping, a scripted label, so keyboard mode only; the operator sees the robot do something unasked; episode time and discard rate rise (10 to 20 percent more steps at epsilon 0.10, calc); the 2.9 cm tall puck is estimated to tip at about 24 to 30 mm push height (estimate in `config.py`, "about 24 to 30 mm for wood on paper", not measured), so tipping from a 5 mm high push is unlikely. Counter-evidence exists but is unverified here: random noise reduced demonstrator success and gave mixed downstream results on three real Franka tasks ([87](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.39054), unverified), and DART-style noise was estimated to cost extra collection time and demonstrator success on a chopstick task ([86](https://arxiv.org/abs/2011.06719), unverified).

What the literature says and where it is silent. No study injects noise into a discrete 1 Hz blocking pipeline; DART's epsilon-greedy discrete form is theory. No head-to-head of start-state randomisation against mid-episode injection was found.

Verdict: secondary. Physical offsets enter the keyboard baseline immediately; epsilon substitution after the first model and the first displacement measurements.

What to correct. Label the perturbed frame with the pressed key, never with the executed step. Replace "0.15 to 0.25 random lateral step" with: physical offsets from episode one; after the first model, epsilon 0.05 to 0.10, then epsilon equal to the held-out disagreement rate, substitutes drawn from the confusion matrix, restricted to moves, never stop, never in the last 3 steps, cooldown of 2 clean steps. Tool: in `__run_steps` draw the substitution from a seeded generator, run the workspace check on the executed step, add `executed` and `perturbed` to `STEP_COLUMNS`, add `perturbation_probability`, `rng_seed`, `perturbed_steps`, `recovery_rule_version` to `episode.json`; the status bar must not reveal the substitution before the next key. Add `--puck-offset-cm`, `--tip-offset-cm`, `--start-arc-cm` written to `episode.json`. Write the recovery rule after the first test's displacement measurements, not from theory. Keep perturbations out of hand-guided replay. List both mechanisms in the method description for the professor.

### 2.8 synthetic_augmentation (training-time picture changes, with or without changed labels)

How it works here. Four different things hide under one name: (1) label-preserving shift and brightness jitter at training time; (2) mirror flip with left and right labels swapped; (3) whole-frame shift relabelled "one step toward the line" (PilotNet style); (4) sheet-plane homography warp with the puck and rod masked, labelled from the demonstrated tip pose 2 to 3 steps later, or learned view synthesis (NeRF, diffusion). Only (1) is in the first training run.

Strengths. Label-preserving random crop or shift is the best-measured training trick in this literature: removing it cost 47 and 35 percent relative on Square and Transport (Square 82.0 to 43.3) and dropped the real Can task from 73.3 to 26.7 percent ([4](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2108.03298)); crop plus mild jitter raised real hand-camera pushing from 62.5 to 87.5 percent and let 50 percent of the data beat 100 percent without augmentation ([10](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2008.04899)); Diffusion Policy's real pipeline is random crop only, at about 0.9 of the side, with a centre crop at inference ([23](https://arxiv.org/html/2303.04137v5), use with care). Corrective view synthesis has the strongest small-data evidence for our sensor and task: 20 to 80 percent with 8 demonstrations and 30 to 100 percent with 24 on eye-in-hand pushing ([3](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.17768), use with care), plus 22.5 points on real grasping (38.75 to 61.25 percent with 14 to 25 demonstrations per object) ([20](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2301.08556)). PilotNet reached about 98 percent autonomy with shifted and rotated views labelled to return to the lane centre within two seconds, and oversampled curve frames ([28](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/1604.07316)); without rotation augmentation NVIDIA's ground robot drifted off after a few tens of metres ([42](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2010.08776)). Our scene is a printed plane with a static capture and one object fixed in the frame, so a faithful geometric augmentation is a homography, not a 3D model (inference).

Weaknesses and severity.
- Fatal for recipe (3): a whole-frame pixel shift relabelled "one step toward the line" is geometrically wrong for a wrist-fixed camera. The puck and rod occupy a fixed place in every frame; shifting them with the line depicts rod-puck contact misalignment, not a puck off the line. The human labels for that appearance are different, so the synthetic labels contradict real frames (inference).
- Fatal for recipe (2) with the stock mount: the camera sits about 2 cm beside the rod axis (line at columns 150 to 200 when aligned, measured), so flipped frames show states that never occur. A 2024 real-vehicle check found flips with steering inversion and 20 percent vertical shifts "did not help" ([43](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.07218)). Mirrored training is reported to need the end effector near the image's vertical centre line ([85](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v305/zhuang25a.html), unverified).
- Major: fixed-frame features and inpainting seams act as shortcut cues; augmentation cannot create puck physics, hairpin manoeuvres, stop contexts or new line shapes; class-prior distortion can make a memoryless model zig-zag; the geometry changes on Monday, so nothing geometric should be built this weekend; rule-based relabelling is a scripted teacher in disguise and bakes in a stepping rule before the rule is written.
- Minor: the SOTA crop spec (160x120 to 144x108, about 8 px) is close to the half-line-width decision threshold (about 9 px at 160 px width, calc); photometric jitter beyond brightness and contrast can erase the red and black cues; every tested variant costs about an hour of robot time; NeRF or diffusion synthesis is infeasible without a GPU and with a moving puck, since SPARTN is limited to static scenes ([20](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2301.08556)).

Verdict: secondary. Label-preserving shift and brightness 0.9 to 1.1 are recommended for the first training run (the gain from label-preserving crop is verified in [4] and [10]; the exact pad-and-shift recipe rests on [82], unverified for details); label-changing augmentation only in the corrected homography form, after bracket F, screened offline and compared in closed loop.

What to correct. Use DrQ-style pad-and-shift at 160x120 (pad 4 px by replicating the border, random crop back; plus or minus 4 px is about 0.2 cm at the puck) that never discards the top rows, instead of the shrink crop ([82](https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.13649), unverified for details: pad size and padding mode not checked against the paper, treat as a working recipe). Brightness and contrast 0.9 to 1.1; no hue or saturation jitter; no blur. Forbid flips on stock-mount data; after bracket F, measure the aligned column and allow flips only if the line centre and puck centroid lie within 320 plus or minus 10 px when aligned, verified on a mirrored sheet placement. If shift-and-relabel is ever used: warp only the sheet plane by a homography fitted from the direction-test frames, keep a fixed puck and rod mask, fill with paper colour, and label from the demonstrated tip position 2 to 3 steps later (the humans' own future action), never from a rule; apply only to forward and diagonal frames; cap synthetic frames at 30 to 50 percent per class. Reframe SOTA report section 2 from "substitutes for demonstrations" to "reduces the demonstrations needed for lateral recovery"; correct the shift recipe, the flip condition and the crop size.

### 2.9 click_target_teleop (click a landing dot or a point on the line instead of pressing a key)

How it works here. A mouse callback on the existing OpenCV window. Variant A: seven on-screen arrow buttons (same labels, slower). Variant B: click the point on the line the puck should head for; the angle from the puck-centre anchor is quantised. Variant C: seven landing dots drawn exactly 1 cm from the anchor; the operator clicks one. The raw click pixel and angle are logged.

Strengths. Removes the mental rotation between picture frame and keys from the operator (the picture frame rotates with the radial camera heading and `picture_left_sign` is untested). Puts the quantisation into code or visible dots, so both operators label with one geometry (inference). Logs a continuous angle, so classes can be re-quantised later without re-recording, and the click can be an auxiliary training target: Sphinx learns from clicked salient points while the policy's input stays the sensor, with 20 to 60 demonstrations per task and 86.7 percent average success ([44](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.05426)). Click-a-target interfaces beat direct control for novices in an HRI grasping study ([90](https://roam.me.columbia.edu/sites/default/files/content/papers/hri2012_grasping.pdf), unverified). One interface for keyboard, replay override and gated rounds. About 60 lines of code.

Weaknesses and severity.
- Major (B): the label depends on how far ahead the operator clicks; on an arc of radius R the direction to a point L ahead deviates from the tangent by about L/(2R) (inference).
- Major: every angle depends on an anchor pixel; a 10 px anchor error at a 75 px radius rotates labels by about 7.6 degrees (calc); the anchor changes with bracket F.
- Fatal if mishandled: overlay contamination of saved frames; draw on a copy only and unit-test byte equality.
- Major: a mis-click on a stop target ends the episode as completed with a false stop label, the most harmful error.
- Minor: window scaling shifts clicks silently (use `cv2.WINDOW_AUTOSIZE`); about 0.5 to 0.9 s slower per step ([91](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keystroke-level_model), unverified); trackpad against mouse; sector-boundary noise in B; no published comparison of click against key labels for discrete behaviour cloning; back, left and right targets fall outside the stock frame because the puck sits at the bottom edge.

Evidence and what the literature says. Interface speed differences of up to two times are real in continuous teleoperation ([21](https://arxiv.org/abs/1811.02790)), but the robot's move caps that effect here (inference). The literature is silent on click against key for discrete labels.

Verdict: secondary (an interface option on top of keyboard stepping; adopt only if the offline agreement test shows clicks at least as consistent as keys).

What to correct. If implemented: variant C with raw-click logging underneath; drop A; keys stay for discard, pause and quit; add `input_device`, `click_u`, `click_v`, `click_angle_deg` to `STEP_COLUMNS`; store anchor and pixels per centimetre per mount in `config.py`; stop requires a confirmation click or Enter, and a stop logged more than 3 cm from the taught line end flags the episode; both operators use a USB mouse; the 29.10 wish-list rule becomes a histogram of `click_angle_deg` after about 50 episodes.

### 2.10 continuous_teleop_then_discretise (gamepad or SpaceMouse driving through jog(), cut into 1 cm chords)

How it works here. Variant A: a thumbstick streams small Cartesian shifts through pyniryo `jog()` (the non-deprecated successor of `jog_pose`; all calls go through the same send-and-wait helper ([59](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/NiryoRobotics/pyniryo/master/pyniryo/api/tcp_client.py))), frames are saved at about 9 per second with the nearest pose, the path is resampled at 1 cm and each frame labelled by the chord direction. Variant B: the gamepad path replaces the hand as the `guided_path` source and is replayed in 1 cm steps as in 2.2. A leader-arm variant needs a second Ned2 and kills the robot's ROS stack, which disables the power button ([46](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/NiryoRobotics/lerobot/main/docs/source/ned2.mdx)), so it is out.

Strengths. Throughput if streaming works: 24 cm at 1.5 to 2 cm/s is 12 to 16 s of driving plus 30 to 45 s of reset, about 50 to 70 episodes per robot hour against about 20 to 30 for keyboard (calc, guess for the rate). The operator drives from the camera window, so demonstration and policy share one observation space (inference, as in [9](https://arxiv.org/abs/1710.04615)). No hand in view, motors on, one person. Arc-length resampling removes operator speed variation and pauses; kinematics-aware resampling raised average success over three real tasks from 47.8 to 71.8 percent against time-uniform downsampling ([47](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.22907), use with care). The hobby line-follower lineage records continuous driving and trains on it ([79](https://docs.donkeycar.com/guide/deep_learning/train_autopilot/)). Exact headings allow later re-quantisation. Timing `jog()` has value for the deployment loop regardless.

Weaknesses and severity.
- Major: whether the Ned2 streams at all through pyniryo is unknown; every call waits for the robot's reply ([59](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/NiryoRobotics/pyniryo/master/pyniryo/api/tcp_client.py)); the robot-side jog timer, shift cap and silence limit come from an unverified config file ([88](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/NiryoRobotics/ned_ros/master/niryo_robot_arm_commander/config/jog_params.yaml), unverified). Nobody has measured the round trip.
- Major (A): mid-motion frames and continuously corrected states are not the stop-and-look states of the deployed classifier; a real modality comparison found replayed smooth demonstrations breaking on the pushing task ([1](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.07017v1)).
- Major (A): frame-to-pose timing unknown ([11](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.10329v3)).
- Major: hardware and build time compete with the method that already matches test time. Novices rate the SpaceMouse badly: SUS 40.5 against 79.1 for VR ([29](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.13042)); 0.63 success against 0.92 for a leader arm and 0.72 for VR with 12 novices ([45](https://arxiv.org/html/2309.13037)).
- Minor: label flicker at class boundaries; motion blur; over-clean data without recoveries; operator reaction lag baked into the path; safety of a stuck stick.
- Fatal: the leader-arm variant.

Verdict: defer. Run the five-minute jog timing test at the first bracket-F session; if the median round trip is 0.15 s or less, build variant B as a one-function swap; drop it for good at 0.4 s or more.

What to correct. Add a `jog` wrapper to `robot_link.py` using `jog()`, not `jog_pose`, and a timing block to the checklist (50 shifts of 2 mm at pushing height). Measure the camera delay once (count how many new frames still change after a move while the pose is constant). If ever adopted, route it as variant B through the replay decider; four safety rules before a puck is on the sheet: 3 mm per tick cap, z recomputed per shift, box check per shift, dead-man trigger. SOTA report section 2 never considers gamepad teleoperation or the jog interface; add one paragraph. Isaac Lab says better devices give better data, not that keyboards are jerky ([24](https://isaac-sim.github.io/IsaacLab/v2.0.2/source/overview/teleop_imitation.html)).

### 2.11 vision_assisted_replay (a colour detector corrects the replay toward the puck or line)

How it works here. Inside the replay decider a red mask finds the puck and a dark mask finds the line above it. Variant A snaps the proposal toward the detected puck or line before the override window. Variant B only pauses the replay and asks the person when the puck or line is lost. Variant C is an offline label checker over stored frames.

Strengths. It attacks the real documented failure of guide-then-replay, open-loop puck drift ([1](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.07017v1); [5](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1604.04038)). Compute cost is a few milliseconds. It yields a per-step puck offset in millimetres, a measurement the project otherwise lacks. Variants B and C produce no labels and are compatible with the brief (inference).

Weaknesses and severity.
- Fatal (variant A): the executed step on a corrected frame is chosen by a colour-threshold controller, which is image-based visual servoing by definition ([89](https://www.irisa.fr/lagadic/publi/publi/Chaumette06a-eng.html), unverified); the frame-label pair teaches the network to copy a classical line follower, and that is not "we push the puck ourselves".
- Major: the detector has no privileged information. The scripted-teacher gains in the literature came from privileged state ([38](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1912.12294)); a same-camera teacher shares the student's blind cone, so the CNN can at best copy a colour threshold.
- Major: blind exactly where help is needed (stock camera beyond 45 degrees); self-crossings and the dashed sheet 06 break a classical line detector; the 400 ms window cannot carry a veto; colour thresholds drift with lamps and the arm's own shadow ([65](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.08191)).
- Minor: only the far half of the puck is in view with the stock mount; bracket-F geometry unmeasured; automation complacency ([36](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/0018720810376055)); time.

Verdict: forbidden or grey as defined (variant A). Variant C (offline checker) and B (pause on lost puck) are acceptable later, after the plain-replay pilot shows whether assistance is needed at all.

What to correct. Do not add an action-choosing detector to `record_episodes.py`. If wanted, build it as an offline script writing `puck_offset_mm`, `line_heading_deg`, `detection_ok`, `cv_expected_action` and a disagreement flag; flagged frames are re-looked at by a person; no label is changed by code. Fix the override window regardless (2.2). Any automatically chosen step on the robot presupposes the direction test on the current mount, the measured neutral column and pixels per centimetre, and a passed offline detection test. SOTA report section 2: a same-camera CV teacher has no privileged information, so the 20-to-100 gain of Learning by Cheating does not apply.

### 2.12 two_operator_consistency_protocol (one written stepping rule, an overlay and an agreement test)

How it works here. A one-page "stepping rule v1" in picture terms: forward while the line's exit point from under the puck lies inside a deadband around the calibrated reference column (stock camera: columns 150 to 200, measured; half a line width is about 37 px, calc); one diagonal when it leaves, then forward again (hysteresis); back only when the puck is more than half a puck off the rod axis; stop only when no line is visible ahead for two consecutive pictures at the end marker (rule for the dashed sheet to be measured); crossings straight through; no hairpin rule under v1. A display-only overlay. Both operators label the same stratified set of frames offline; Cohen's kappa gates collection. Operators alternate every five episodes; `rule_version`, `operator`, `camera_mount`, `outcome` and `discard_reason` go into `episode.json`.

Strengths. It targets the best-documented cause of degradation in multi-human imitation data ([2](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2306.02437), use with care; [4](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2108.03298)); a second operator's demonstrations can pull success below the single-operator baseline unless made compatible first ([81](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.08073), unverified). Human teachers "can be inconsistent and make mistakes" ([77](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.00600)). Our task is nearly unimodal, so the inconsistency surface is small and nameable (inference). Seven classes make agreement measurable with kappa before any training, which continuous data cannot offer. The tool already stores operator, commit and per-step source. The rule doubles as the override rule for correction rounds. Balanced, interleaved operators keep each student's share auditable (inference). Mixing demonstrations recorded at different tempos is reported to lower success on a real Push-T task ([47](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.22907), use with care: the paper frames this as operator mixing within a resampling study, not as an operator-consistency study; the specific drop quoted in earlier drafts, 48.6 to 22.2 percent, could not be verified and is not used).

Weaknesses and severity.
- Major: the rule as drafted in the SOTA report is in table coordinates the operator cannot see; it must be in display pixels with an overlay.
- Major: the replay decider labels by a different policy (nearest geometric action, no deadband), so mixing keyboard and replay episodes mixes two label policies for the same pictures; the modality-mixing gain in [1](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.07017v1) came from one demonstrator.
- Major: the agreement check is easy to do wrongly. Forward dominates, which inflates raw agreement and destabilises kappa under prevalence; offline relabelling differs from in-the-loop choices (correlations 0.22 to 0.60 in [22](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1610.00850)). The kappa thresholds (below 0.60 inadequate, 0.80 to 0.90 strong) and sample sizes (about 126 to 335 items for two raters depending on prevalence) come from sources not checked here ([83](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3900052/), [84](https://academic.oup.com/ptj/article/85/3/257/2805022), unverified); the thresholds below are therefore working values, not verified requirements.
- Major: everything calibrated this weekend is void on Monday.
- Major: the hairpin manoeuvre and crossing rule cannot be written yet.
- Major: "discard episodes with reversed steps" removes the only positive examples of the back class; no per-step mistake flag exists. Deep networks tolerate label noise only with a significant increase in dataset size ([76](https://arxiv.org/abs/1705.10694)), which we do not have.
- Minor: operator confounded with sheet or lighting; rule drift over seven weekends; a rigid rule narrows recovery variety (pair it with offsets); the stop criterion is ambiguous on dashed sheet 06; the cited evidence comes from continuous teleoperation by four to six operators, so the size of the effect here is inference.

Evidence. As above, plus the compounding bound that multiplies every inconsistent label by the horizon: T squared epsilon for behaviour cloning ([13](https://arxiv.org/abs/1011.0686)).

Verdict: primary (the mandatory process layer under whichever collection method is chosen).

What to correct. Restate every threshold in pixels with a display-only overlay calibrated per mount with a ruler (puck centred, 5 mm and 10 mm each side); derive the deadband from the measured puck displacement after one diagonal step (section 6). Make the replay decider implement the same rule or keep replay data as a separate flagged source. Agreement check: 150 to 200 frames stratified so no class dominates (about equal per moving class, all back and stop frames, half from each operator), blind relabelling in an offline mode that reuses the keyboard keys, kappa with a confidence interval, per-class agreement and a count of opposite-direction disagreements; at least 0.6 to start recording, at least 0.8 before correction rounds (inference; the threshold conventions and the 150 to 200 sample size come from [83] and [84], unverified); repeat every 50 episodes and after any rule change. Replace "discard reversed steps" with a frame-level rule (drop flagged mistakes and their undo steps; keep prescribed back steps). Alternate operators every five episodes, 40 to 60 percent per operator per sheet. Issue rule v2 for hairpins and crossings only after a no-puck dry run with bracket F. SOTA report section 2 asserts two students "will look like one proficient operator" without proposing a measurement, and cites the robomimic 78.0 against 66.0 result without saying it holds for BC-RNN only (plain BC went the other way, 52.7 against 58.7, [4](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2108.03298)).

### 2.13 iterative_small_seed (train after 30 to 40 episodes, then record, train, test and correct every session)

How it works here. A schedule, not a modality. Once bracket F passes its direction test and a reference picture is stored, the first session records 30 to 40 keyboard episodes over at least three local shapes (sheet 01, sheet 02, the gentle part of sheet 03 or a printed gentle S), 10 to 12 per shape, with varied starts, offsets and a quarter of episodes started 5 to 8 cm before the line end. The model is trained the same evening (minutes on a laptop at 1,000 to 10,000 frames, guess), an ungated sanity test of 5 starts follows, and every later session adds keyboard episodes or a gated round, with retraining from scratch on all data.

Strengths. Time to first signal drops from two weekends to one robot hour, so pipeline faults (sign, stale frame, crop, 30 stop examples, label disagreement) surface on 7 to 8 November before 100 episodes depend on them. On-policy frames are worth more per frame than extra demonstrations in every real-robot comparison found ([8](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1810.02890); [18](https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.06733); [17](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.08416); [19](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.07953); [39](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1709.07174); all but [17] and [19] use with care). The two reasons the literature gives for a large seed (rollout safety, poor retroactive labels) do not apply to a 1 cm step confirmed from the picture (inference). DAgger's own seed is one iteration's worth of expert data, about 1,000 samples ([13](https://arxiv.org/abs/1011.0686)); IWR and Sirius start from 30 demonstrations ([18](https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.06733); [17](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.08416)); LeRobot suggests at least 50 episodes with 10 per location and "avoid adding too much variation too quickly" ([7](https://huggingface.co/docs/lerobot/en/il_robots)). RaC's 6 to 10 short rounds beat batched collection ([19](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.07953)). Schema changes are cheap while little data exists. The override rate per round is a built-in progress metric.

Weaknesses and severity.
- Major: a seed that is too narrow makes the first policy sample the wrong states; HG-DAgger chose a 10,000-label seed because the novice shapes the sampling ([8](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1810.02890), use with care).
- Major: automation bias in gating ([36](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/0018720810376055)).
- Major: class imbalance is worse in a small seed (about 30 stop frames).
- Major: dataset consistency across six to eight sessions (lamps, sheet position, bracket seating, the operators' evolving reading of the rule).
- Minor: a false first signal read as "we need more data"; operational overhead across two laptops (the training script and policy decider do not exist yet; session folders collide); the override rate is a biased proxy for autonomy; no study compares seed sizes at a fixed budget for a 1 Hz discrete policy; a larger share of the final dataset consists of robot-proposed, human-confirmed steps (disclosure).

Evidence. Plain behaviour cloning (BC-RNN, image) on the robomimic subsets: about 40 demonstrations gave 83.3 percent on Can and 29.3 on the precision task Square; about 100 gave 97.3 and 64.7; 200 gave 98.0 and 82.0 ([4](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2108.03298)).

Verdict: primary (the schedule the rest of this note uses).

What to correct. Replace "record 100 to 150 episodes before the first training" (SOTA report section 2 and the 9 to 22 November row) with "train after the first 30 to 40 episodes and after every session that adds 20 or more"; keep the 200 to 300 total as the floor. Freeze stepping rule v1 and the seven-class set before the seed and version them. Build and test the training script, a dataset manifest check (class counts, duplicate `frame_md5`, missing frames) and the policy decider on the simulator output before 2 November. Fix the session-folder collision before the first merge. Round stopping rules: end a round at 300 override frames or one third of the seed's frames, whichever is earlier; when the override rate on seen sheets is below 5 percent for two rounds, spend the budget on new printed sheets. Reference-pose picture at the start of every session; line centre within about 10 px of the previous session's picture (inference) or re-teach.

### Alternatives rejected, with reasons

- Hand-guided replay as an equal primary source: open loop on the puck; the pushing task in the real modality comparison [1] is where replay broke ([1](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.07017v1)); hides a mirrored sign; costs about 1.5 times the robot time per episode (calc).
- Raw kinesthetic frames as training data: five independent mismatches with test pictures; hand-in-view analogues scored near zero ([31](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.01974v1); [49](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.21864)).
- Trace-then-replay, a scripted line teacher and an action-choosing colour detector as label sources: labels not a function of the picture; outside "we push the puck ourselves"; no privileged information to justify the detector.
- A large seed of 100 to 150 before the first training: copied from a vehicle-safety setting that does not apply; delays the first signal by two weekends.
- Continuous teleoperation before the jog timing test: unknown feasibility; mid-motion frames; the leader-arm route needs a second Ned2 and a killed ROS stack.
- Click input before the baseline: a real consistency benefit but no published policy gain; the baseline must exist first.
- Whole-frame shift-and-relabel and flips on stock-mount data: geometrically wrong.
- NeRF, diffusion or Gaussian-splat view synthesis: static-scene limit, GPU, a moving puck ([20](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2301.08556)).
- ACT or Diffusion Policy as the primary model: they solve multimodality and smoothness problems a 1 Hz discrete step does not have (SOTA report section 1); keep as an optional comparison.

### Not analysed in this pass

The completeness check at the end of the writing found approaches that none of the thirteen critiques covered. They are listed so that nobody mistakes silence for rejection; none has been weighed against the evidence yet.

- Rendering synthetic training frames of the printed sheet, or co-training with the simulator that `record_episodes.py --simulated` already contains (Wei et al. 2025 [48] report real-sim co-training for pushing). The simulator's puck physics are rough and unmeasured, so this would be a picture-only aid at most.
- Self-supervised recovery data in the MILES style: after a human demonstration the robot moves itself a few millimetres off each waypoint and records the way back. Related to 2.7, with the robot instead of the operator producing the offset; the rules question of 2.5 applies.
- Waypoint or keyframe demonstrations (Akgun et al. 2012 keyframes; "Waypoint-Based Imitation Learning", Shi et al. 2023): the operator marks a few points along the line and the tool interpolates the steps. Close to 2.4 and 2.9; labels would not come from a picture-by-picture human choice.
- Niryo's own hand-guided trajectory recording (NiryoStudio's learning-trajectory recorder, pyniryo's saved trajectories), which logs joints rather than the tip and could replace `__record_guided_path`.
- The Vision Set camera plugged into the laptop (32 pictures per second measured, against 9 through the robot) for any mid-motion variant.
- A hand-held pusher carrying the camera, UMI-style, so that a person pushes the puck directly while the camera sees what the robot would see; it needs a second rig, and the pictures would not come from the robot's own loop.
- Pooling demonstrations with other groups working on the same six sheets: grading compares groups, so probably not allowed; ask before assuming.
- The kappa thresholds (0.6, 0.8) and the sample sizes in section 6 rest on general agreement-statistics conventions ([83], [84]) that were not verified in this pass; treat them as working values.
- No human-time budget exists yet for the tool changes in section 2 and the training script; section 5 budgets robot hours only.

Why these numbers (inference, anchored on the literature). The curves for real pushing from pixels give about 50 demonstrations for half success and about 150 for full success at one goal with randomised starts ([48](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.22634)); the scaling study recommends about 50 per environment-object pair ([15](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.18647)); LeRobot's floor is 50 episodes with 10 per location ([7](https://huggingface.co/docs/lerobot/en/il_robots)); the easy robomimic tasks reached 83 to 100 percent with about 40 demonstrations and the precision task needed 100 to 200 ([4](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2108.03298)). Treating the whole line-following skill as one task with the sheets as its variations, 280 initial episodes plus 120 correction episodes sit inside those ranges, with the curved sheets weighted higher because every analogue found that curve frames must be oversampled ([28](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/1604.07316)) and that corrections repair drift on the shapes they run on. The mapping from "task" to "sheet" is an inference.

The first "does the pipeline work" model needs 30 to 40 episodes over three local shapes (10 to 12 each) with the offset and near-end quotas, so that every moving class has at least 5 percent of frames and the stop class has at least 30 frames (inference; under the tool's current logging each completed episode yields exactly one stop frame, so 30 to 40 episodes give at most 30 to 40 stop frames; near-end starts raise stop frames per robot hour, not per episode); about 1.5 to 2 robot hours (calc). Expect 20 to 50 percent success on sheet 01 ungated (inference from behaviour cloning at 8 to 24 demonstrations giving 20 to 30 percent in [3](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.17768), use with care, and from 10 demonstrations giving 2 of 20 in [48](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.22634)). Its pass criteria are those of a pipeline check, not a performance estimate: held-out top-1 accuracy of at least 70 percent against a 14 percent chance level, at least 1 of 5 ungated trials finishing sheet 01, and no trial in which left and right are mirrored. Below that, suspect the sign, the crop, the stop class or the freshness guard before recording more.

The guide-then-replay pilot is sized for a decision, not for data: 10 episodes per mode per operator on sheet 01 (rehearsal) and 10 per mode on sheet 02 (bracket F) give override and discard rates and times with enough resolution to apply the gates in section 6, but not enough to separate policy success between modalities (20 trials cannot resolve 13 of 20 from 14 of 20). How the tests settle the two open decisions. Decision 1 (hand-guided method) is settled by the step 5 thresholds: keep guide-then-replay as a comparison arm if fidelity, override and discard pass; keep only the assisted-keyboard variant if overrides exceed 30 percent; drop it if the safety test fails or the time ratio is 1.5 or more at equal completion. Decision 2 (class set) is settled by the matched-start, agreement and histogram tests: the seven classes stay unless opposite-direction disagreements or the wish-list notes show a recurring, camera-visible situation; the diagonal displacement number fixes the deadband and the hysteresis; the stop class is tied to a visible line end, and an explicit end action matters (in one imitation benchmark an explicit Done action padded onto every demonstration was critical, with success falling to about 15 percent without it, [80](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2002.00412)).

## 7. Questions for the professor

1. Hand-guiding modes. Hand-guided demonstrations will be replayed by the robot in 1 cm steps with a student confirming or overriding every step, because raw hand-guided pictures are not usable for a camera-only policy. Does he regard the replay, and the confirm-by-key variant in which the hand path is only an on-screen proposal, as "pushing the puck ourselves"?
2. Correction rounds. The robot will execute steps proposed by our own trained model that a student approves one by one before they move; every training label stays human-chosen or human-approved and no reward or reinforcement learning is used. Is that within "we push the puck ourselves"?
3. Perturbation. May the recording tool occasionally execute a deliberately randomised 1 cm step that the operator then corrects (DART-style noise injection), and may episodes start with deliberate puck or rod offsets, with every label still a human key press?
4. Scripted geometry. Is an offline script that checks human labels against the taught line geometry acceptable as a quality check, given that it produces no training labels? Would he allow a puck-aware geometric labeller for states the trained model visits, declared as such and compared against a human-only model, or does he want labels to be human only?
5. Printed sheets. May the team print its own training sheets of the same size and line thickness, and in what number? Will the unseen evaluation sheets come from the same SVG family (curvature radii, crossing angles, dashed lines), and can a dashed line appear among them?
6. Line ends. May a small end marker be printed on training sheets, and will the evaluation sheets have a visible line end, so that the stop class is tied to something the camera sees?
7. Grading. How exactly will "finishing the track" be judged (puck centre within one puck radius of the line end?), and when does the clock start and stop?
8. Held-out sheet. May one provided sheet be kept out of training as a held-out test, or should all six be trained on?
9. Step length. Finishing time is ranked. Is a 2 cm forward step (measured 1.07 s against 0.77 s for 1 cm, `config.py`) acceptable as an action, if it is demonstrated and learnt like the others?
10. Hairpins and loops. If the measured blind-frame fraction on sheets 05 and 06 stays above 10 percent with bracket F, is a well-measured partial result on the observable sheets an acceptable course outcome, or should the team spend December on memory and repositioning manoeuvres?
11. Input devices. Does a gamepad or a mouse click count as "keyboard driving", should the jog timing make it worthwhile?
12. Reportable comparison. Would he accept a short written comparison of keyboard against guide-then-replay on sheets 01 and 02 (20 trials per mode) as a reportable result? No paper found in this pass compares hand-guiding with discrete keyboard stepping for a classification policy.
13. His own experience. In his thesis, were hand-guided trajectories recorded kinesthetically or by motion capture, and were pictures part of the data? This decides how his "hand-guiding was faster" experience transfers.
14. Robot time. Are weekday sessions possible in weeks 46 to 50 and during the Christmas window, and is a second person always required at the robot? The stretch budget needs about 3 to 4 weekday hours per week.

Corrections to the SOTA report that this note relies on, for the record: (a) "only because the recorded poses were replayed" overstates [1]; (b) the HG-DAgger behaviour-cloning counts are 1 collision and 6 road departures (Table II of [8]), not 11 and 66; (c) Sirius zeroes about 15 robot actions, about 2 s of human reaction, not 22 s ([17]); (d) Belkhale's 200-episode Square value at sigma 0.3 is 50.7, not 54.3 ([2]); (e) the Kim et al. paraphrase misstates the mechanism ([33]); (f) the robomimic "78.0 against 66.0" holds for BC-RNN only ([4]); (g) the whole-frame shift-and-relabel recipe is geometrically wrong for a wrist camera; (h) "hand in view and blurred frames" is untested for our geometry; (i) the Learning-to-Drive 21 to 25 percent transfer figures may belong to the reinforcement-learning agents, not the imitation baseline (unverified; recheck before it appears in a deliverable); (j) the modality mixture in [1] beat the best single modality by about 5 percent, not by 20 percent on average.
