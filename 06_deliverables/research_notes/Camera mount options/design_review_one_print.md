# Design review: the one print, and whether it should be the collar on the rod (option E1)

Date: 26.10.2026. For: Emranul and Marshal (team M, project 6). Deadline: 10.01.2027.

This note merges five independent reviews of the proposal in `option_geometry.md` (option E1: a printed collar on the rod carrying the camera, panned by joint 6). The reviewers looked at it from five angles: mechanical clearance against Niryo's own 3D files, the collar and its mounting, the optics, the control software and the learning, and a sceptical overall comparison with option F and the no-print paths. Nothing in this note was measured on your robot by the reviewers; everything they computed is marked as such, and the only photos of your robot they had are the ones in your scratchpad and `figures\setup_2026-10-25_overhead.jpg`.

**How to read the number tags.** Every number carries its origin:

- **[team]** measured by you on 24./25.10 (ruler, pictures, timings, meeting notes).
- **[Niryo]** taken from Niryo's own files or pages (STEP, STL meshes, URDF, ROS source, datasheets, docs). Files are in `niryo_files\`.
- **[calc]** calculated by a reviewer from Niryo's files, your pictures, or the validated kinematics model. Reliable only as far as its inputs are.
- **[assumed]** a guess a reviewer had to make because nobody has measured it. These are the dangerous ones.
- **[model]** a number from the proposal's own coverage model (`cam_options.py` etc.), which checked only the lens point.
- **[speculative]** a reviewer's subjective estimate.

**Short glossary.** *Hand face* = the top end of the silver rod, where it comes out of the wrist; it is also the origin of joint 6. It sits 59.5 mm above the paper while pushing [calc: 5 mm magnet gap (your rule) + 26 mm block below the rod end [team] + 28.5 mm rod length [Niryo STEP]]. *Fork cheeks* = the two blue arms at the end of the forearm that hold the wrist between them (joint 5 runs through them). *Panel bulge* = the box on one side of the forearm end with the FreeMotion / Save / Custom buttons. *Pan* = turning joint 6 so a camera on the rod looks in another direction. *Look-ahead* = how far along the line the camera sees beyond the puck.

---

## 1. Verdict

No: E1 as drawn must not be the one print, and all five reviewers reached that conclusion independently. The proposal placed only the lens point and checked it against a wrist drawn as a 2.3 cm cylinder; the real wrist [Niryo STEP and STL] is a 36 x 52 x 69 mm box sitting inside a two-cheeked fork at the end of the forearm, and the cheeks hang about 5 mm below the hand face, 23 to 30 mm either side of the rod [calc from the meshes]. A real camera body (face about 37 x 37 mm, depth somewhere between 15 and 30 mm [assumed from photos; never measured by anyone]) with its lens 3 cm out at the hand-face height sits between those cheeks and hits them at almost any pan: the free pan is somewhere between zero and about +-5 degrees for the thinnest body any reviewer assumed, and the +-120 degrees the proposal relies on does not exist [calc]. Lowering the camera under the cheeks drives it into the puck (top 29 mm [team]) and the magnet block; the only placements that clear everything give 3 to 4 cm of look-ahead with the puck's top filling the lower half of the picture, which is worse than today's camera [calc]. On top of that, the +-120 degree software limit leaves an 80 degree blind wedge that does not fit the unused heading gap of the two loop sheets [calc], the pan is not free in time (the hairpins need up to 75 degrees of pan per 1 cm step, making sheets 03 to 06 take 31 to 119 percent longer [calc]), and the control software for a pan (pan rule, reachability guard, singularity guard, collision recovery, camera-frame labels) is about a week of new code for beginners [speculative]. What would make a hand-mounted camera right: a clamped prototype on the real robot that clears the housing, the fork and the block through +-120 degrees with the puck's leading edge in the lower third of the picture and at least 5 cm of line ahead; a pose command with a yaw change that plans and costs at most about 1 s per step; and a cable that survives 20 full pans without moving the collar. The reviewers think all three together are unlikely with the Vision Set camera, but finding out costs one afternoon and no print. What flips the decision to F (tall wrist bracket, no pan, no software change, 89 to 98 percent of the hard sheets observable in the proposal's own model [model]): the prototype failing any of the three, which is the expected outcome; F then needs its own body-level check, because its camera body is 1 mm into the forearm at tip x = 0.14 m and clear from x = 0.16 m [calc]. What flips it to the staged path (record with a non-printed clamp mount now, send the print later as a copy of a geometry that has already produced good pictures): the professor allowing a non-printed mount, and the Maker Space confirming that several parts in one file count as one print. The recommended move today is the staged path with F as the default: measure, prototype, test the software with the stock camera, and send the file only when it reproduces a position that has already been photographed working.

## 6. Where the reviewers disagree, so you are not confused by it

- **Is pan 0 itself free at the E1 spot?** The mesh-sweep reviewer finds +1.5 mm at pan 0 for a 15 mm deep body (and a collision for 20 or 25 mm); the sceptical reviewer, adding a 12 mm lens barrel, finds a collision at every pan angle. Both depend on the unmeasured body depth (Step 1 G). Nobody finds a usable pan.
- **Joint-6 speed at the 20 percent cap:** 36 deg/s from the ROS joint_limits.yaml [Niryo] versus about 20 deg/s from the docs' 1.775 rad/s and your 0.6 s per 20 degree move [team]. T2 in Step 2 measures it.
- **Refocus:** one reviewer measured your frames and finds the ring set near 6 cm (no refocus for E1-type heights, one refocus to 12 cm helpful for F); two reviewers computing with an assumed aperture expect a refocus for both. The no-touch sweep (Step 1 N) decides; nobody touches the ring before it.
- **Option C:** "no-print fallback that can be tried this week" versus "a demo for the report, not a plan". Both agree it is not the final-run plan.
- **The optics-compatible hand mount (short boom, lens 6 to 8 cm from the rod axis at 7 to 8 cm height, tilt 0 to -12, pan +-120):** proposed by the optics reviewer as the only hand geometry whose picture would work; its clearance, boom stiffness, cable and base-foot swing at x below 0.17 were not checked by anyone and the coverage model has not been run for it. It is not a candidate for the one print unless it goes through Steps 1 to 3 like everything else.
