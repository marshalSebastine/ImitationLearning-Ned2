# Camera options for the puck-pushing task: geometry, coverage and ranking

> **Withdrawn in part, 26.10.26 evening.** Option E1 (collar on the rod, panned by joint 6) was refuted by the design review in `design_review_one_print.md`: this study placed only the lens point and drew the wrist as a 2.3 cm cylinder, but the real forearm fork's cheeks hang about 5 mm below the rod's top on both sides, so a camera body beside the rod cannot pan, and lowering it puts it into the puck. The E1 and G1 rows below are lens-point artefacts. Current ranking: F (tall wrist bracket, needs its own body-level check at tip x below 0.16 m), then C, then A.

Date: 26.10.26. Model: the validated Ned2 kinematics (`ned2_fk.py`, 0.6 mm worst case against recorded poses) plus a pinhole camera calibrated on the team's pictures of 25.10.26. Every number below is **computed (model)** unless marked **measured (team)**. Scripts to re-run everything are listed at the end; the full tables are in `option_geometry_tables.md` next to this note.

## 1. Result in one paragraph

With the camera where it is (option A) the picture covers push headings from about 42 degrees to the right to 30 degrees to the left of "straight away from the base" (54 to 72 degrees in all, depending on whether the lens is a 64 or a 52 degree one) and the line ahead is visible for 7 cm; the best orientation of the sheets leaves 35 to 55 percent of sheets 03 to 06 observable, so the policy would be blind through every hairpin. Leaning the rod towards the base (B) makes the picture smaller, not larger, because the lens swings outward as the rod leans; rolling the forearm (C) gives nearly full coverage but only while the tip is 17 to 24 cm from the base. A printed collar on the rod that carries the camera 3 to 4 cm beside the magnet block and turns with joint 6 (option E1, a pan of plus or minus 145 degrees) makes all six sheets fully observable with 8 to 11 cm of line ahead at every heading except a 24 to 30 degree wedge pointing straight back at the base. A tall printed wrist bracket without pan (F) reaches 89 to 98 percent of sheets 03 to 06 with no new action. Ranking for "finish first, then be fast": E1, then F, then C, then A; B and D are worse than A. The measured window is consistent with a 52 degree lens 1 cm above the wrist axis or, equally, with Niryo's published 64 degree lens 1 cm below it (the bracket photo favours the latter); a 104 degree lens would have to sit 3.6 cm above the paper, in front of the puck, which the bracket cannot do.

The team summarised this as "8.5 cm wide at the puck, 10 cm at the top, from the puck centre to 7 cm beyond it".

How well it fits (52 degrees): the pixel fit reproduces the line edges to 2 px and the puck's far edge at row 346 (measured 346 to 356); the window it implies is 2.9 to 10.3 cm from the rod axis, 8.2 cm wide at the bottom and 10.6 cm at the top, against the team's 1.9 to 8.9 cm and 8.5 / 10 cm: the far edge comes out 0.4 to 1.0 cm beyond the wood-edge evidence (9.3 to 9.9 cm from the rod axis), the widths within 0.6 cm. The lens position agrees with Niryo's URDF (camera 2.3 cm ahead of the rod axis at wrist-axis height, tilt 10 degrees) to within 1 cm and 10 degrees; the pixel data prefer the steeper tilt. The model also reproduces the 20 degree lean picture: with the recorded joints of the lean the optical axis is 2 degrees from vertical (measured: the line's edges parallel) and the lens 8.4 cm above the paper (measured line width 82 px, i.e. 7.9 cm for a 52 degree lens).

A third field of view had to be added after the sibling note `niryo_data.md` turned up Niryo's own camera intrinsics (ned_ros `cam_intrinsics.yaml`: fx 508 px for 640 x 480, strong barrel distortion k1 = -0.41): they give a pinhole field of **64.4 degrees** horizontally (about 75 degrees to the raw picture border once the distortion is undone). Fitted with 64.4 degrees the same pixel data are matched just as well (2.1 px RMS, 5.0 px on the check run) with the lens 4.1 cm ahead of the rod axis, 0.9 cm below the wrist axis and 7.0 cm above the paper, tilt 15.6 degrees; the 20 degree lean picture is matched slightly better than with 52 degrees (line width 83 / 81 / 79 px predicted against 83 / 81 / 81 measured, versus 78 / 78 / 78 for 52 degrees). The pictures alone therefore cannot tell "52 degrees with the lens 1 cm above the wrist axis" from "64 degrees with the lens 1 cm below it"; the bracket photo (lens level with the lower edge of the wrist housing) fits the lower position, so 64 degrees is at least as likely as 52. For options A to D this makes no difference, because the model is calibrated to the pictures either way; for the new mounts E and F a 64 degree lens gives windows about a quarter larger than a 52 degree lens at the same height, so both are reported.

One fitted quantity to note: the puck centre sits **2.9 cm** from the rod axis in the pushing pictures (fit; prior was 2.1 cm = block body radius 0.9 cm plus puck radius 1.2 cm; the task's 1.85 cm is magnet radius plus puck radius). Either the puck runs 0.5 to 0.8 cm ahead of the block after each step, or the lens is that much further back than fitted; the two are indistinguishable in the pictures. All puck-relative numbers below use 2.9 cm and carry about plus or minus 0.8 cm of uncertainty from this.

Why 104 degrees is excluded: Niryo's own intrinsics say 64 degrees (75 to the raw border), and to show a window only 8.5 cm wide at the puck, a 104 degree lens would have to be 3.6 cm above the paper and 4.8 cm ahead of the rod axis, i.e. below the rod end and beyond the puck's leading edge; the stock bracket holds the lens level with the wrist housing, 8 to 9 cm above the paper. Placed there, a 104 degree lens would show 25 cm across at the bottom row, three times the measured 8.5 cm. The 104 degree rows in the appendix are therefore meaningless for A to D (the model cannot even see the puck from that position) and are given for E and F only as "if the lens really were 104 degrees".

## 4. What each option does, and what it costs

### 4.1 A: current bracket, rod vertical (baseline)

- Window (model): from 2.9 cm to 10.3 cm ahead of the rod axis, i.e. from the puck centre to 7.4 cm beyond it, 8.2 cm wide at the bottom and 10.6 cm at the top; the same at every tip radius because a vertical rod fixes the wrist orientation. Measured (team): 8.5 / 10 cm wide, 7 cm beyond the puck.
- Headings (52 degree lens): the line ahead is visible for 2 cm from -48 to +33 degrees, the puck's leading edge from -42 to +30 degrees; together -42 to +30 (72 degrees). With the 64 degree lens, which sits 2 cm lower, the puck's leading edge leaves the picture at -33 and +21 degrees (54 degrees in all); the truth is between the two and a one-picture test with the puck turned 30 degrees to either side would settle it. The asymmetry is the line sitting in the left third of the picture: turns to the right are seen 12 degrees further than turns to the left, so a sheet should be laid so that its tight turns go to the right.
- Look-ahead: 7.2 cm straight ahead, 2.0 cm at 45 degrees, nothing beyond.
- Sheets: 01 fully, 02 45 to 57 percent, 03 to 06 35 to 55 percent at the best orientation (64 to 52 degree lens).
- Costs: none. Software: pose commands as now. Risks: none new; the policy is blind through every hairpin and loop.

### 4.2 B: rod leaned towards the base by joint 5 (joint 4 held at 0)

- The lean rotates the camera down (optical axis 9.4 / 0.7 / 10.6 degrees from vertical at 10 / 20 / 30 degrees of lean; at 20 degrees it looks straight down, which is the "parallel line edges" the team saw) but it also carries the lens outward (4.0 / 4.8 / 5.5 cm ahead of the rod axis instead of 3.0) and lower (8.3 / 7.5 / 6.6 cm above the paper). The window therefore shrinks: 2.4 to 8.7, 2.0 to 7.5, 1.6 to 6.6 cm from the rod axis; its near edge moves 0.5 to 1.3 cm behind the puck centre, which is the only gain (the puck sits a little higher in the picture). Covered headings fall to 66 / 60 / 48 degrees, the look-ahead to 5.8 / 4.5 / 3.5 cm, the observable fractions of sheets 03 to 06 to 37 to 45 percent.
- Pusher contact (model, rod end held in place): the magnet tip moves 0.45 / 0.89 / 1.30 cm towards the base and rises 0.04 / 0.16 / 0.35 cm; the lowest point of the tilted magnet rim stays within 0.07 cm of the present clearance, so pushing outward still works and the block body (not the magnet) touches the puck 0.5 to 1.5 cm above the paper. Pushing back towards the base the leaning block overhangs the puck by 0.7 / 1.1 / 1.6 cm at the puck's top, so the first contact is at the puck's top edge (2.9 cm): a taller-than-wide puck pushed at its top tips over. The wrist housing's bottom shifts 0.9 / 1.9 / 2.9 cm towards the base, still above the puck.
- Costs: no hardware. Software: joint commands (joint 5 lean with joint 4 at 0 and the tip corrected through forward kinematics), because pose commands change joint 4 during the lean (team, 25.10.26). Re-teaching: all demonstrations with the lean. Risks: the tipping contact when pushing back, a smaller window than now, and the 25.10 lesson that pose commands drift the twist. Verdict: not worth it on its own.

### 4.3 C: forearm roll (joint 4) as a camera yaw with the rod within 8 degrees of vertical

- Band (model): the forearm is vertical at tip x about 0.20 m; between x = 0.18 and 0.22 m the full roll of plus or minus 115 degrees keeps the rod within 5.4 degrees of vertical and turns the camera by up to 117 degrees either way. Outside the band the allowed roll falls fast: 40 degrees at x = 0.26 (camera yaw 20 degrees), 35 at 0.14 (yaw 53 degrees, the base turn adds yaw there), 20 at 0.30, 15 at 0.34, 10 at 0.38 to 0.42; beyond x 0.30 the camera turns by only 2 to 12 degrees, the slightly tilted poses still widen the covered band to 84 to 108 degrees.
- Coverage, union over the roll band: 297 degrees (-153 to +141) at x 0.18 to 0.22, 150 at 0.26, 135 at 0.14, 84 to 108 beyond 0.30. Added over A: 222 degrees in the band, 9 to 75 elsewhere. The window itself is unchanged (2.9 to 10.4 cm along the camera's azimuth); the tip stays on the line to 0.01 mm because joints 1, 2, 3 and 5 are re-solved for every roll.
- Sheets: 02 fully, 03 79 percent, 04 67, 05 and 06 69 percent, because the tight turns can be placed in the band only one at a time.
- Costs: no hardware. Software: joint commands with the roll as one more action dimension and the tip corrected through the kinematics (a pose command cannot express the roll with a vertical rod). Re-teaching: all demonstrations. Risks: the rod tilts up to 8 degrees in the rolled poses (contact height on the puck moves by up to 0.4 cm), the band is only 6 cm long, and an unseen track puts its hairpins where it likes.

### 4.4 D: B plus C

- With joint 4 rolled, joint 5 can no longer lean the rod towards the base: at every radius except 0.14 m the requested lean and roll leave a rod-direction error of 7 to 21 degrees, so the solver has to give up one of them. At x = 0.14 a 10 degree lean with plus or minus 35 degrees of roll is feasible (rod tilt 10.8 degrees, coverage 138 degrees). Everywhere else D reduces to B, and B is worse than A. Not recommended.

### 4.5 E: printed mount on the hand link (rotates with joint 6)

Design E1 (chosen): a collar on the bare rod above the magnet block carrying the Vision Set camera with its lens 3 cm beside the rod axis, level with the bottom of the wrist housing (hand origin, 5.9 cm above the paper), looking 32 degrees from straight down along the collar's forward direction. The tilt was chosen so the puck's leading edge sits at row 380 to 430 of 480 (lower fifth) when the pan points along the push; with the lens 4 cm out (E1b) the tilt that does this is 14 degrees. Payload: camera about 30 g plus a printed collar well under 50 g against the 300 g limit; not an issue.

- Window with the pan along the push: 4.3 to 10.6 cm from the rod axis (1.4 to 7.7 cm beyond the puck centre), 5.6 cm wide at the bottom and 8.9 at the top (narrower than now because the lens is lower). With the 64 degree lens (tilt 35 degrees): 4.0 to 13.4 cm, 6.9 / 13.6 cm wide, look-ahead 11.2 cm, coverage -168 to +168 degrees.
- Heading coverage: joint 6 is limited to plus or minus 145 degrees. Because the lens sits 3 cm from the rod axis in the pan direction, at a pan of 145 degrees it stands beside the puck when the push points at the base, so the puck leaves the picture: covered -165 to +165 degrees at every radius, a 30 degree blind wedge pointing straight back at the base (E2: 48 to 72 degree wedge, because its higher lens also sees the forearm). Look-ahead 8.2 cm at 0, 45, 90 and 135 degrees, 0 at 180.
- Occlusion when pushing back towards the base: none by the rod or the block, because the pan carries the lens to the base side of the rod, where the rod is behind the lens; none by the forearm for E1 (the lens is below the wrist and looks under it; the model gives identical coverage with and without the forearm). For E2 (lens 10 cm above the paper) the forearm cuts the backward view and, worse, the lens is 0.3 to 1.0 cm inside the forearm's envelope at pans beyond about 120 degrees towards the base at x 0.22 to 0.42 m: E2 as drawn would collide. An arm-up design needs the lens 5 cm or more from the rod axis.
- Clearance of E1 (model, forearm radius 3 cm and housing radius 2.3 cm assumed): 0.7 cm from the wrist housing at pan 0, 0.0 cm from the forearm envelope at pan 145 degrees; the lens stays 3.0 cm above the puck's top when swinging over it. With the lens 4 cm out (E1b) the clearances become 1.7 and 0.8 cm at the price of a shorter look-ahead (5.2 cm) and a 36 degree wedge. Either print the collar with the camera 4 cm out, or keep 3 cm and limit the pan to plus or minus 120 degrees in software (coverage then about plus or minus 150 degrees).
- Sheets: all six 100 percent observable (best orientation: 01 at 0 degrees, 02 at 15, 03 at 75, 04 at 120, 05 and 06 at 30 reversed; every orientation keeps the blind wedge off the path).
- Time cost of the pan: the pan travel equals the sheet's turning: 57 degrees on sheet 01, 165 on 02, 510 on 03, 939 on 04, 688 on 05 and 06. At the team's measured 0.6 s per 20 degree joint move that is 1.7 / 5.0 / 15 / 28 / 21 s if every pan is a separate move, on top of 19 / 21 / 42 / 60 / 44 s of pushing at 0.77 s per centimetre. If the pan is sent in the same command as the step (a pose command with its yaw, or a six-joint move), both run together and the extra time is close to zero because a 20 degree joint-6 move (0.6 s) is shorter than a 1 cm step (0.77 s).
- Costs: hardware: a printed collar plus camera holder (STEP/STL to the Maker Space); the Vision Set cable must allow plus or minus 145 degrees of twist below the wrist. Software: one more action (joint 6, or the yaw of the pose command); the recorder must log it. Re-teaching: every demonstration, with the new view. Risks: the collar's grip on the 2.5 cm bare rod with the block snapped on (check the free rod length first; clamping on the block itself is possible but the block is the end effector, so ask the professor); the camera body passing 3 cm above the puck during a pan; the clearance at full pan (above); the policy must learn to pan, which hand-guided demonstrations do not teach unless the pan is driven by a rule (point the camera along the last heading).

### 4.6 F: printed mount on the wrist link, higher and tilted, no pan

- Grid search (lens 2 to 8 cm ahead of the wrist origin, 0 to 8 cm above the wrist axis, tilt -10 to +50 degrees) for the largest mean observable fraction with the puck's leading edge in the picture. Best for 52 degrees: lens 5.1 cm ahead of the rod axis, 8 cm above the wrist axis (15.9 cm above the paper), tilt -10 degrees (slightly back towards the base), i.e. nearly straight down from high up. Window -4.1 to 7.9 cm from the rod axis (7.0 cm behind the puck centre to 5.0 cm beyond it), 16.8 cm wide at the bottom and 14.8 at the top. Covered headings -117 to +117 (234 degrees); look-ahead 5.0 cm straight ahead, 7.5 at 45 degrees, 5.0 at 90. The next best designs: 6 cm up (-105 to +105), or 4 cm ahead and 8 up with tilt 0 (-96 to +96). With the 64 degree lens the same coverage (-117 to +117, sheets 0.98 / 0.90 / 0.89) is reached with the stalk only 6 cm above the wrist axis (lens 13.9 cm above the paper), which eases the clearance; for a 104 degree lens the best is 7.1 cm ahead, 4 cm up, tilt -10 (-126 to +126, lens 11.9 cm above the paper).
- Sheets: 01 and 02 fully, 03 98 percent, 04 90, 05 and 06 89 percent: only the tips of the hairpins (headings beyond 117 degrees from radial) are blind, for a few centimetres each.
- Picture quality: from 15.9 cm the 1 cm line is about 41 px wide (now 64 to 75) and the puck about 100 px; still plenty for a 640 x 480 policy input.
- Clearance (model): the lens is 0.8 cm outside the forearm's envelope at x = 0.14 m, where the forearm leans outward over the bracket, and 3.6 cm or more beyond x = 0.22 m; the bracket must be a stalk rising 8 cm above the wrist axis on the outward side, clear of the forearm's swing (14.6 degrees inward at x 0.14 to 48 degrees outward at x 0.42; it never crosses the stalk's side in the pushing poses, but this was checked only at the lens point).
- Costs: hardware: a printed bracket replacing the stock one (the camera's own screws can be reused). Software: none (pose commands, rod vertical, no new action). Re-teaching: every demonstration. Risks: the stalk's stiffness and the camera shaking on it; the cable; collision of the stalk with the forearm at x below 0.14 m or with anything above the table; the view behind the puck shows the rod, the block and the housing from above (the model accounts for the hidden patch under them: it is why the coverage stops at 117 degrees).

### 4.7 G: combinations

- G1, E2 plus a 10 or 20 degree lean towards the base: the lean tilts the hand camera further down and moves it outward; coverage -153 to +153 at x 0.14 and -144 to +144 elsewhere, look-ahead 6 to 13 cm; sheets 97 to 100 percent. No gain over E1, plus the tipping contact when pushing back (section 4.2) and the collision problem of E2.
- G2, F plus a 20 degree lean: -120 to +120, sheets 89 to 100 percent, 1 to 2 points better than F, with the tipping contact. Not worth the lean.
- The combination that does pay is E1's pan with the rod kept vertical: it keeps the proven pushing geometry and adds only the one joint the pusher does not use.

## 5. Ranking for "finish the unseen track, then be fast" (hairpins and loops expected)

1. **E1, printed collar on the rod with the camera panned by joint 6.** The only option whose coverage (333 to 336 degrees, look-ahead 8 to 11 cm at every covered heading) makes every provided sheet fully observable, and the blind wedge is short enough (30 degrees, a few centimetres of a hairpin) to bridge with the policy's history. Cost: one print, one more action, re-recording. Time cost: zero to 30 percent depending on whether the pan rides inside the step command. Print the camera 4 cm out, or limit the pan to 120 degrees.
2. **F, tall wrist bracket, no pan.** 89 to 98 percent of the hard sheets observable with no change to the control scheme or the action space; the policy sees 7 cm behind and 5 cm ahead of the puck, which also makes the drift (1 cm per 19 cm, measured) visible. Weaker only at the very tip of hairpins. Choose F over E1 if the team wants to keep pose commands and a two-dimensional action.

## 7. Method and assumptions

- Arm model: `ned2_fk.py` (Niryo URDF chain, validated 25.10.26). Inverse kinematics with joint limits in `camgeo.py` (scipy least squares, several starts, elbow-up branch).
- Camera: pinhole, 640 x 480, 4:3 with square pixels, horizontal field of view 52 or 104 degrees; mounted on the wrist link (A to D, F, G2) or the hand link (E, G1). Lens position in the mount frame with the rod vertical: forward (radially away from the base), up, right; tilt from straight down towards forward; yaw.
- Table: the plane taught on 25.10.26. Pushing height: magnet tip 5 mm above the paper, rod end 2.6 cm higher, wrist axis 7.9 cm above the paper at x 0.26 m.
- Puck: 2.4 cm wide, 2.9 cm tall, centre 2.9 cm from the rod axis in the push direction (fitted, see section 2).
- Occluders: the puck, the magnet (radius 0.65 cm), the block (0.9 cm), the rod (0.9 cm), the wrist housing (radius 2.3 cm, assumed) and the forearm (radius 3 cm, assumed) as cylinders; a line point counts as visible only if its line of sight misses all of them.
- Criteria: "line ahead visible" = at least 2 cm of a straight line from the puck centre in the push direction visible within 20 cm; "puck visible" = the puck's top-rim point in the push direction inside the picture with a 12 px margin and not hidden. Headings scanned every 3 degrees, tip radii 0.14 to 0.42 m every 4 cm.
- Sheets: the six photos traced (`cam_sheets.py`, same thresholding and thinning as `trace_sheets.py`, with the walk continued across the loop crossings), scale from the 1 cm line width, resampled every 2 mm, heading from a 1.2 cm baseline. Each sheet's bounding-box centre placed at x = 0.28 m, y = -0.02 m, rotated in 15 degree steps, traversed from either end; points the arm cannot reach (rod radius outside 0.13 to 0.42 m) count as not observable (none occurred at the best placements). At each point the local heading decides observability using the option's coverage set at the nearest tip radius; for C and D the policy may use any roll in the band, for E any pan within the joint-6 limits (the pan with the longest visible line is chosen per heading). The curved line ahead is approximated by the local tangent, so the hairpin tips are slightly optimistic for every option alike.
- Not modelled: lens distortion, the camera body's own size (clearances are for the lens point), the forearm's swing against the F stalk away from the lens point, the dashes of sheet 06 (a 2 cm visible stretch may fall in a gap).
