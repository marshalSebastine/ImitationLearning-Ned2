arm; never push on a held arm; no manual calibration; write down what was measured and what was guessed; stop when the list is

## How the parts screw together

There are only two joints, and both use holes that already exist.

1. **Bracket to robot.** Today Niryo's black holder is fixed by two hex screws that go into two threaded holes in the aluminium brace inside the wrist. The new bracket's base plate uses those same two holes. The stock screws are probably too short for a plate under them, so step 3 measures their thread and length, and you buy the same thread 5 to 10 mm longer (or the Maker Space supplies them). Nothing new is drilled into the robot.
2. **Camera to bracket.** The camera stays in Niryo's holder. The holder's two ears have through-holes; the bracket's top pad gets two slots at that spacing with a printed pocket behind each for an M3 nut. Two M3 bolts with nylon-insert nuts hold the holder to the pad. The slots give a few millimetres of height adjustment; a hinge or detents give the tilt.

So: brace, then the bracket's base plate on the stock holes with longer screws, then the stalk, then the top pad, then Niryo's holder on two M3 bolts, then the camera in its holder as today. The cable runs the same way it does now.

What can still go wrong is only measurement: the thread of the brace screws and the ear spacing are not published anywhere, so both come from step 3 with calipers, and the 1:1 paper test in step 8 catches a mistake before the print.
