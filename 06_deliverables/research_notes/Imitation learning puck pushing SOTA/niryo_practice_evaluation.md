# Niryo Ned2 in practice: prior learning work, control interface limits, wrist camera, and evaluation protocols for path-following pushing (as of October 2026)

Use case carried through every judgement below: Ned2 + pyniryo 1.2.5 over Ethernet (blocking moves, roughly one 1 cm tool step per second, one TCP client at a time), Vision Set wrist camera only (640x480 JPEG via `get_img_compressed`, about 9 frames/s measured by the team), red 2.4 cm puck pushed along a 1 cm black line on A4 to the line end, imitation learning only, final test on an unseen path sheet, two beginner master's students, due 10 January 2027.

Note on sources: docs.niryo.com was migrated to a JavaScript-rendered platform (Docsie) and every direct fetch of a docs.niryo.com or archive-docs.niryo.com page returned only the page shell. Where an official-docs statement is cited below, the wording comes from the search-engine excerpt of that page and should be re-checked in a browser before being quoted in the report. The pyniryo source on GitHub was fetched directly and is reliable.

## Key question 1: Has anyone run imitation learning (BC, ACT, Diffusion Policy, LeRobot) on a Niryo Ned/Ned2, and with what interface, rate and problems?

### Takeaway
Yes, but only through one official Niryo x Hugging Face integration (2025-2026) that bypasses pyniryo and ROS entirely: it kills the ROS stack, exposes the robot's Dynamixel/stepper motor bus over TCP, and drives it from a PC with a second Ned2 as a leader arm, recording at 15 fps. No published thesis, paper, or student write-up of imitation learning through pyniryo on a Ned/Ned2 was found; the only other learning-based Ned2 code found (UniROS) is a reinforcement-learning environment driven through ROS trajectory topics.

### Cited Findings
- LeRobot pull request #1778 "[NIRYO x HF] Making Ned2 Available in LeRobot" (author atphantson, from NiryoRobotics/lerobot) adds `ned2` follower and `ned2_leader` teleoperator support; the stated test workflow was "record a dataset, train and evaluate a policy" with two Ned2 arms, using `lerobot-record --robot.type=ned2 --teleop.type=ned2_leader` with `--dataset.fps=15` and link-local IPs `169.254.x.y`; PR description dated 22 August 2025, labelled by a maintainer 17 October 2025, closed unmerged 4 August 2026 because of CLI conflicts, failing CI and staleness, with the maintainer asking that third-party robots now ship as standalone plugin packages via `register_third_party_plugins` — [huggingface/lerobot PR #1778](https://github.com/huggingface/lerobot/pull/1778)
- The PR's file list shows the integration lives in `src/lerobot/robots/ned2/` (`ned2.py`, `config_ned2.py`, `configure_all_steppers.py`) and `src/lerobot/teleoperators/ned2_leader/`, and adds a `"stepper"` model (model number 2000, resolution 4096) to LeRobot's Dynamixel control tables; the docs state the aim is to "minimize latency between PC and robots for real-time control (processing on PC, execution on hardware)" — [PR #1778 files](https://github.com/huggingface/lerobot/pull/1778/files)
- The Ned2 driver in Niryo's fork uses `DynamixelMotorsBus` on a socat virtual serial device linked to `TCP:<robot-ip>:7777` (not pyniryo and not ROS); joints 1-3 are steppers (motor IDs 2, 3, 4), joints 4-5 are `xl430-w250` (IDs 5, 6), joint 6 and the gripper are `xl330-m288` (IDs 7, 11); `get_observation()` does `sync_read("Present_Position", num_retry=10)` plus `cam.async_read()`, and `send_action()` optionally clamps with `max_relative_target` before `sync_write("Goal_Position", ...)`; calibration needs interactive `input()` and joint range limits are hard-coded per unit — [ned2.py in NiryoRobotics/lerobot](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/NiryoRobotics/lerobot/main/src/lerobot/robots/ned2/ned2.py)

- Niryo's GitHub organisation hosts a `lerobot` fork (last updated 31 October 2025) and a `lerobot3.0` fork (last updated 20 February 2026), alongside `pyniryo` (updated 22 May 2026) and `ned_ros` (updated 1 July 2026) — [github.com/niryorobotics](https://github.com/niryorobotics)
- Niryo Academy lists a course pairing the Ned2 with Hugging Face LeRobot, described in the search excerpt as covering teleoperation-based dataset recording, imitation learning and policy training with prerequisites of basic Python and access to a Ned2; the page itself is behind a login — [NiryoAcademy course 78](https://academy.niryo.com/enrol/index.php?id=78)
- Niryo's FAQ lists LeRobot as a possible integration for advanced AI projects alongside YOLO and TensorFlow — [Niryo FAQ](https://niryo.com/resources/faq/)
- UniROS provides a real-Ned2 Gymnasium environment (`NED2RobotEnv`) that commands the arm by publishing `JointTrajectory` messages to `niryo_robot_follow_joint_trajectory_controller/command` or via MoveIt with `async_move=True`, reads `/joint_states`, optionally reads the wrist camera from `/niryo_robot_vision/compressed_video_stream`, interpolates trajectories at 100 points per second, and includes a safety check rejecting per-joint deltas above 0.15 rad; it does not use pyniryo — [UniROS ned2_robot_real source](https://uniros.readthedocs.io/en/latest/_modules/rl_environments/ned2/real/robot_envs/ned2_robot_real.html)
- Other public Ned2 student/community projects use classical vision rather than learning: a two-robot pick-and-place system with YOLO, OpenCV and PyNiryo including workspace calibration and image-to-robot coordinate mapping — [SeunFilaoye/Niryo_two_robot_system](https://github.com/SeunFilaoye/Niryo_two_robot_system); a robot-vs-robot tic-tac-toe project that deliberately avoided deep-learning detectors as too heavy for lab laptops — [Hadifard/niryo-ned2-robot-tictactoe](https://github.com/Hadifard/niryo-ned2-robot-tictactoe)
- Comparable low-cost arm, ACT/ALOHA (2023): the policy "directly predicts joint positions at 50Hz", learns from "50 demonstrations for each task", and the abstract reports 80-90% success on real tasks from about 10 minutes of demonstration data; the paper names "compounding errors" and non-stationary human demonstrations as the core difficulties — [ACT project page](https://tonyzhaozh.github.io/aloha/); [ACT paper, RSS 2023](https://www.roboticsproceedings.org/rss19/p016.pdf)
- Comparable low-cost arm, 2026: an imitation-learning study of suture following on an open-source arm (SO-101 per the search excerpt) trained 28 models of ACT, Diffusion Policy, SmolVLA and pi0 on 160 teleoperated demonstrations (32,374 frames) and reports 50-75% task success under ideal conditions with "depth error" as the dominant failure mode across all architectures; the search excerpt describes a wrist camera and a fixed side camera each streaming RGB at 10 Hz — [arXiv 2605.28736](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.28736)
- A Hugging Face forum thread (9 August 2026) from a user training ACT on 50 episodes with two webcams reports the recording loop running at about 5.6 Hz against a 30 Hz target and the policy consistently missing to the right or only reaching demonstrated positions; the reply advises that coverage matters more than episode count, to evaluate well-covered and held-out positions separately, and that a consistent directional miss points to calibration, camera placement or timing rather than data quantity — [HF forum thread](https://discuss.huggingface.co/t/what-is-the-expected-success-rate-for-a-act-trained-model-using-50-episodes/178517)
- A 2026 benchmark of VLA policies on the SO-101 reports that "execution-related failures, particularly repetition loops and grasp instability" are common across policies and that the imitation-learning baseline shows comparatively higher state-mismatch rates — [arXiv 2606.08881](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.08881)

### Inferences
- The only working Ned2 imitation-learning stack on record is incompatible with this project's constraints: it needs a second Ned2 as leader, a custom gripper, SSH changes on the robot, and killing ROS (which disables the power button). With one robot, a wrist-only camera and beginner students, the pyniryo route is the realistic one, and the team should expect to be the first documented group doing BC through pyniryo on a Ned2 (no counter-example was found).
- The 15 fps used by Niryo x HF and the 10-20 Hz typical of ACT/Diffusion/UMI deployments are 10-20x faster than the team's ~1 step/s, so published rates cannot be matched; the design instead has to be a quasi-static "one picture, one 1 cm step" policy. This is defensible for a pushing task because the puck does not move between steps (inference, not a cited result).
- The forum case (5.6 Hz loop, consistent rightward bias) is the closest analogue to a slow pyniryo loop and suggests that timing and calibration bugs, not data quantity, should be checked first when the policy drifts.

### Gaps
- No thesis, paper, blog post or YouTube demo of behaviour cloning, ACT or Diffusion Policy on a Niryo Ned/Ned2 via pyniryo was found in any search (arXiv, GitHub, general web). The Niryo Academy LeRobot course content is behind a login and could not be read.
- Whether Niryo's `lerobot3.0` fork (February 2026) already repackages Ned2 as a plugin per the maintainer's request could not be confirmed.
- No reported success rates, latency figures or failure analyses exist for the Niryo x HF integration; PR #1778 gives only the 15 fps setting.

## Key question 2: What control rates and latencies does pyniryo / the Niryo TCP server allow, and are there documented faster paths a student team could use safely?

### Takeaway
The TCP server (port 40001) is documented as single-client and strictly sequential: it answers only after a command finishes, so pyniryo cannot stream commands and the per-step rate equals the robot's own execution time per move. Faster paths exist (jog without planning, `execute_trajectory_from_poses` for multi-waypoint moves, ROS jog service / JointTrajectory topic on the robot, or the LeRobot direct-bus route), but none has a documented rate, and the ROS and direct-bus options require working on the robot's Linux system.

### Cited Findings
- Niryo's TCP server documentation (ROS stack v3.2.0 page): "Only one client can communicate with the server (reconnection effective but no multi clients)"; "The server answers only after the command is done, so it can't deal with multiple commands at the same time"; "To access the server, you will have to use to robot's IP adress and communicate via the port 40001" (quotes from search excerpt; page now JS-rendered) — [Use Ned's TCP server (v3.2.0)](https://docs.niryo.com/dev/ros/v3.2.0/en/source/tcp_server.html); same wording in [v3.1.2](https://docs.niryo.com/dev/ros/v3.1.2/en/source/tcp_server.html)
- pyniryo client constants: `TCP_PORT = 40001`, `TCP_TIMEOUT = 5`, `READ_SIZE = 512`; command codes include `SET_ARM_MAX_VELOCITY = 3`, `SET_JOG_CONTROL = 4`, `MOVE_JOINTS = 20`, `MOVE_POSE = 21`, `MOVE_LINEAR_POSE = 23`, `JOG_JOINTS = 25`, `JOG_POSE = 26`, `MOVE = 29`, `JOG = 30`, `EXECUTE_TRAJECTORY_FROM_POSES = 83`, `EXECUTE_TRAJECTORY_FROM_POSES_AND_JOINTS = 84`, `EXECUTE_TRAJECTORY = 90`, `GET_IMAGE_COMPRESSED = 200` — [pyniryo enums_communication.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/NiryoRobotics/pyniryo/master/pyniryo/api/enums_communication.py)
- pyniryo client implementation: after connecting, the socket is set to `settimeout(None)` (blocking); `__send_command` sends a packet and `__receive_answer` waits for the reply with no read timeout; `move_pose`, `move_joints`, `move_linear_pose`, `jog_joints`, `jog_pose` and `execute_trajectory_from_poses` are all marked deprecated since 1.2.0 in favour of `move()` with a `PoseObject`/`JointsObject`; `jog_joints` docstring: "Jog corresponds to a shift without motion planning"; `jog_pose` arguments are `[dx, dy, dz, d_roll, d_pitch, d_yaw]` in metres/radians; `set_jog_control`: "Set jog control mode if param is True, else turn it off"; `set_arm_max_velocity`: "Limit arm max velocity to a percentage of its maximum velocity", range 1-200; `execute_trajectory_from_poses` takes a "List of [x,y,z,qx,qy,qz,qw] or list of [x,y,z,roll,pitch,yaw]" with a `dist_smoothing` parameter; no control-loop rate or frequency appears anywhere in the file — [pyniryo tcp_client.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/NiryoRobotics/pyniryo/master/pyniryo/api/tcp_client.py)
- The Python ROS wrapper (runs on the robot) exposes `jog_joints_shift`, `execute_trajectory_from_poses` and `execute_trajectory_from_poses_and_joints`, and the state topics `/joint_states` and `/niryo_robot/robot_state` (search excerpt) — [Python ROS Wrapper documentation (v3.2.0)](https://docs.niryo.com/dev/ros/v3.2.0/en/source/python_ros_wrapper/ros_wrapper_doc.html)
- The arm commander package has a `jog_timer_rate_sec` parameter described only as the publish rate for the jog controller (no default shown in the excerpt), a `JogShift` service with `JOINTS_SHIFT = 1` and `POSE_SHIFT = 2`, under namespace `/niryo_robot_arm_commander/` — [niryo_robot_arm_commander docs](https://docs.niryo.com/dev/ros/source/stack/high_level/niryo_robot_arm_commander.html)
- Niryo's joystick tutorial drives the arm through the jog service `/niryo_robot/jog_interface/jog_shift_commander`, states the Jog Interface moves the arm "without using motion planning", and warns that learning mode must be deactivated or jogging will not work — [Control Ned/Ned2 with a Keyboard or a Joystick through a ROS Node](https://docs.niryo.com/applications/ned/v1.0.4/en/source/tutorials/control_ned_ros_node_joystick.html)
- UniROS commands the real Ned2 by publishing `JointTrajectory` messages to `niryo_robot_follow_joint_trajectory_controller/command` and interpolates at 100 points per second of trajectory time (default 0.5 s), i.e. an alternative on-robot path that bypasses the TCP server — [UniROS ned2_robot_real](https://uniros.readthedocs.io/en/latest/_modules/rl_environments/ned2/real/robot_envs/ned2_robot_real.html)
- The LeRobot route replaces the TCP server with a raw motor bus: `ser2net` exposes `/dev/ttyAMA0` on TCP 7777 at 1,000,000 baud, the ROS stack is killed, and the PC drives the Dynamixel/stepper bus directly; link-local addressing is used "for low latency" — [ned2.mdx](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/NiryoRobotics/lerobot/main/docs/source/ned2.mdx)
- The robot's vision ROS node publishes `/niryo_robot_vision/compressed_video_stream`, exposes a stream compression quality parameter and a subsampling factor, and a start/stop streaming service (search excerpt) — [niryo_robot_vision (v3.1.2)](https://docs.niryo.com/dev/ros/v3.1.2/en/source/ros/niryo_robot_vision.html)
- pyniryo image docs: "As sending raw images will lead to heavy packets which can saturate the network, it sends compressed images"; the client returns "a string containing a JPEG compressed image" to be decoded with `uncompress_image` (search excerpt; also in tcp_client.py) — [Image processing overview](https://docs.niryo.com/dev/pyniryo/source/vision/image_processing_overview.html)
- Ned2 connectivity: Ethernet 1 Gb/s, Wi-Fi 2.4/5 GHz, USB 2.0/3.0; collision detection by Hall-effect sensor — [robots.ros.org/ned2](https://robots.ros.org/ned2/)
- Ned2 joint speed: the 2025 Niryo specification sheet lists "Joint 1 : 45°/s" while an earlier listing shows "Joint 1 & 3 : 90°/s" (search excerpt; the PDF itself is image-only) — [Ned2 technical specifications PDF](https://niryo.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/10001874-4_TECHNICAL_SPECIFICATIONS_NED2.pdf); [docs.niryo.com technical specifications](https://docs.niryo.com/product/ned2/source/hardware/technical_specifications.html)

### Inferences
- The team's measured ~1 step/s is consistent with the documented design (server replies only after the move completes, MoveIt planning per `move_pose`); it is not a bug the students can fix on the PC side.
- `jog_pose` / `set_jog_control` skips motion planning and is the one pyniryo call most likely to shorten the per-step round trip, but there is no published number; it must be timed empirically (the memory note on "test before hardware changes" applies). Jog is deprecated in 1.2.x but still present in the command table, so it works with 1.2.5 unless removed later.
- `execute_trajectory_from_poses` lets one blocking call execute a multi-waypoint path, which is useful for replaying a demonstration smoothly but not for closed-loop image-conditioned control (the camera cannot be read mid-call because the server is busy).
- On-robot ROS paths (jog service, JointTrajectory topic, the Python ROS wrapper) could raise the rate but require SSH, ROS Noetic knowledge, and risk leaving the robot in an unusable state; given beginner students and a fixed deadline these are high-risk, and the single-client constraint means they also cannot run in parallel with a pyniryo connection.
- The ~9 fps image rate is plausibly dominated by request-reply round trips (each `get_img_compressed` is a blocking request) rather than the camera itself; no official frame-rate figure exists to compare against.

### Gaps
- No official latency or maximum command rate is documented anywhere for pyniryo, the TCP server, jog mode or `execute_trajectory`; the default value of `jog_timer_rate_sec` was not visible in the excerpt.
- The current docs.niryo.com pages could not be fetched (JS-rendered); the quoted TCP-server statements come from the v3.1.2/v3.2.0 page excerpts and should be re-checked against the Ned2 docs version in use.
- No Niryo forum thread about pyniryo loop rates surfaced in search (forum.niryo.com did not appear in results).

## Key question 3: Known Vision Set wrist-camera facts (FOV, mounting, intrinsics, frame rate, latency) and community notes on using it for learning

### Takeaway
The best available figures are a Sony IMX322 colour sensor, 640x480 in robot mode, roughly 104 degrees field of view and about 3 mm focal length (from Niryo's login-gated academy course), with intrinsics and distortion coefficients retrievable from the robot via `get_camera_intrinsics`; no official frame-rate or latency figure exists, and Niryo's own troubleshooting page attributes stream latency to network quality and recommends Ethernet.

### Cited Findings
- Niryo Academy course "From Camera to Grasping with the Niryo Ned2" (course 57, listed as last updated 21 August 2026) gives the onboard wrist camera as an IMX322 (CMOS) sensor, 640 x 480 resolution, field of view about 104 degrees, focal length about 3 mm, and a pixel size of 12.8 x 11.6 micrometres (search excerpt; page requires login) — [NiryoAcademy course 57](https://academy.niryo.com/course/view.php?id=57)
- Retail listing for the NED Vision Set: "Sony IMX322 colour camera", lens 2.1 mm, maximum resolution 1080p (1920 x 1080), connects through the Ned2 back panel via USB 3.0 (search excerpt) — [RobotShop Niryo NED Vision Set](https://jp.robotshop.com/en/products/niryo-ned-vision-set)
- RS Components lists the Ned2 Vision Set as "220 mm Colour Vision Set - 1920x1080" with detection range 220 mm, and the Niryo One version with detection range 150-500 mm, both 1920 x 1080 — [RS 2412218](https://nz.rs-online.com/web/p/vision-sensors/2412218); [RS 2052814](https://nz.rs-online.com/web/p/vision-sensors/2052814)
- pyniryo `get_camera_intrinsics` returns the stored "camera intrinsics, distortions coefficients", reshaped to a 3x3 matrix and a distortion vector; `get_img_compressed` returns a JPEG string — [pyniryo tcp_client.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/NiryoRobotics/pyniryo/master/pyniryo/api/tcp_client.py)
- Niryo Vision Set troubleshooting: the Vision Set "requires a good network quality to show a livestream. If you are using a Wi-Fi network, it may overload it, generating some latency between the commands and the robot reaction"; suggested fixes are disabling the stream in Niryo Studio (switch to the 3D view tab) or connecting over Ethernet (search excerpt) — [Warnings & Typical issues](https://docs.niryo.com/product/vision-set/source/warnings_typical_issues.html)
- The robot-side vision node offers a compression-quality parameter and a subsampling factor on `/niryo_robot_vision/compressed_video_stream` (search excerpt) — [niryo_robot_vision](https://docs.niryo.com/dev/ros/v3.1.2/en/source/ros/niryo_robot_vision.html)
- The academy vision course pipeline is calibration, then object detection and grasping, using solvePnP for 6D pose and hand-eye calibration, with the workspace defined by visual markers (search excerpt) — [NiryoAcademy course 57](https://academy.niryo.com/enrol/index.php?id=57)
- Ned2 arm specification relevant to image-to-motion precision: repeatability "ISO 9283: +/- 0.5 mm", payload 300 g at a 20 mm TCP, reach 440 mm (docs; the 2025 sheet says 490 mm) — [robots.ros.org/ned2](https://robots.ros.org/ned2/); [Ned2 technical specifications PDF](https://niryo.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/10001874-4_TECHNICAL_SPECIFICATIONS_NED2.pdf)
- UniROS reads the Ned2 wrist camera for learning from the compressed ROS stream and decodes it to RGB; it is disabled by default and readiness is checked only by topic type, not by receiving frames — [UniROS ned2_robot_real](https://uniros.readthedocs.io/en/latest/_modules/rl_environments/ned2/real/robot_envs/ned2_robot_real.html)

### Inferences
- With a ~104 degree field of view, the horizontal footprint at height h is about 2h x tan(52 deg) = 2.6h; at 15 cm above the sheet the view spans roughly 38 cm, so the whole A4 sheet (29.7 x 21 cm) fits in frame from the team's "camera looks ahead along the arm" pose. This is a computation from an unverified login-gated figure, not a measured fact; a quick ruler test at the working height would settle it.
- The listed pixel size (12.8 x 11.6 micrometres) is physically implausible for an IMX322 (a 1/2.9-inch 1080p sensor) and is probably a transcription error in the course page; treat the FOV figure with the same caution until measured.
- The 2.1 mm (retail) versus ~3 mm (academy) focal-length disagreement cannot be resolved from sources; `get_camera_intrinsics` from the robot gives the effective focal length in pixels and is the number to use.
- Because every pyniryo image is a request-reply JPEG over the same single TCP connection as the move commands, image reads and move commands are serialised; capturing the frame after each blocking move completes (arm static) removes motion blur and observation-action lag at the cost of speed.
- Ned2 repeatability (0.5 mm) is 20x smaller than the line width (10 mm) and 48x smaller than the puck (24 mm), so arm precision is not the limiting factor; perception and policy are.

### Gaps
- No official datasheet with FOV in degrees, frame rate, exposure control or latency for the Vision Set was found; the Vision Set manual page at docs.niryo.com/accessories/vision-set/ could not be fetched (JS-rendered).
- Whether pyniryo exposes exposure / white-balance controls for the wrist camera could not be determined from the client source fetched (no such command was seen in the enum excerpt).
- No community notes specifically on using the Niryo wrist camera for policy learning were found beyond the UniROS code and the Niryo x HF PR, neither of which reports camera latency.

## Key question 4: Evaluation protocols for path-following / trajectory-tracking manipulation and line-following robots: success definitions, trials per condition, unseen layouts, reporting

### Takeaway
The field's norm for real-robot imitation learning is a pre-registered binary success criterion (object within a tolerance zone), 20-25 trials per condition with randomised but documented initial conditions, per-subtask or progress metrics alongside success, and raw counts with interval estimates rather than bare percentages; line-following work adds lateral deviation (mean, median, max) and distance/fraction completed, with 20-50 runs per path and unseen paths chosen by holding out whole layouts.

### Cited Findings
- Kress-Gazit et al. (2024), best practices for policy evaluation: report "the number of evaluations performed across each condition and not just percentages"; "Providing a point estimate for success and performance metrics can be misleading"; "there must be a clear, detailed, and unambiguous definition of success" written in advance; define and report initial conditions, matching them visually with image overlays or scene markings; run all policies in one session so lighting and environment are similar; state whether evaluation conditions were chosen in- or out-of-distribution; use rubrics recording sub-goal results per rollout (a policy with 17% overall success still flipped the pancake in 100% of runs); describe failure categories with frequencies; worked example: 13/20 vs 14/20 could not be distinguished under a uniform-prior binomial model, and a policy scored 0/8 after a lab reorganisation versus 13/20 before — [arXiv 2409.09491](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.09491v1)
- STEP (Snyder et al., RSS 2025): sequential testing reduces required trials by up to 32% versus fixed-sample baselines while keeping statistical power; adding trials after seeing results and re-testing "amounts to p-hacking" (search excerpt) — [arXiv 2503.10966](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2503.10966)
- ACT (RSS 2023): simulated tasks evaluated with "3 seeds and 50 policy evaluations each", real-world tasks with "1 seed and 25 evaluations"; every real task object "is randomized along the 15cm white line"; results are reported per subtask stage (e.g. Thread Velcro "success rate decreased by roughly half at every stage, from 92% success at the first stage"); teleoperation success in Table I is "the number of successes out of 10 trials"; chunk-size ablation peaks at k = 100 — [ACT paper PDF](https://www.roboticsproceedings.org/rss19/p016.pdf)
- Diffusion Policy (2023), real Push-T: success is defined as "end-state IoU greater than the minimum IoU in the demonstration dataset" (i.e. the threshold is taken from the demos, not chosen arbitrarily); 20 evaluations per method; maximum 600 rollout steps; results reported as success rate, average IoU and duration (DP end-to-end CNN: 0.95 success, 0.80 IoU, 22.9 s vs human demo 1.00, 0.84, 20.3 s) — [arXiv 2303.04137 (HTML v5)](https://arxiv.org/html/2303.04137v5)
- D3IL benchmark (2024): a pushing trial is successful "when both blocks are positioned inside a predefined target zone, with a certain level of tolerance allowed" (search excerpt) — [arXiv 2402.14606](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.14606v1)
- Survey of imitation learning for manipulation (2025): success is "typically measured by binary task completion and goal distance thresholds", with completion time and pose/placement accuracy as companion metrics (search excerpt) — [arXiv 2508.17449](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.17449v1)
- VAT-Mart (2021): a trajectory counts as successful "if the specification is met within a tolerance threshold of 15%" because exact targets are rarely hit (search excerpt) — [arXiv 2106.14440](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2106.14440)
- For continuous-contact pushing where a terminal goal is ill-defined, LessMimic (2026) reports "the proportion of time steps during which the end-effectors maintain active contact with the target object" plus root-trajectory tracking error (search excerpt) — [arXiv 2602.21723](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.21723)
- A hierarchical IL paper (2026) separates completion from success: "A trial is counted as Task Success iff Episode Completion is achieved and Execution Quality is at least 2" (search excerpt) — [arXiv 2608.03103](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.03103)
- AI Driving Olympics lane following (2018): metrics were median lateral deviation from the centre line, travelled distance, survival time and time outside the driveable zone (search excerpt) — [arXiv 1903.02503](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1903.02503)
- SAC-PID line following (2021): a trial is successful when the robot reaches its goal without leaving the line; 20 trials on each of several paths, success rates 80-100%; paths of different difficulty, tested in Gazebo and on a real mecanum robot (search excerpt plus abstract) — [arXiv 2103.10686](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.10686)
- LineMaster Pro (2026, educational line follower): 200 controlled trials; mean tracking accuracy 1.18 cm at 0.4 m/s with 95% CI [1.06, 1.30]; per-condition statistics (mean lateral error, SD, min/max, 95% CI) over 50 runs per condition measured with a high-speed camera; 94% recovery from path deviations — [arXiv 2603.13907](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.13907)
- A DRL lane-following paper computes lateral and orientation deviation, integrates them over time and folds them into a weighted composite score (search excerpt) — [arXiv 2304.08235](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2304.08235)
- Trial counts in recent real-robot IL papers vary: 20 trials per task, 10 per task, 15 per task, RoboMIND "tested each model ten times" (search excerpts) — [RoboMIND](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2412.13877); a robotics course sets ">= 70% success across 20 live evaluation trials" as its deployment benchmark — [roboticscenter.ai unit 5](https://roboticscenter.ai/learn/paths/linker-bot-o6/unit-5-train-deploy)
- A policy-evaluation harness notes that the field "almost universally reports bare success rate from a handful of trials with no statistical analysis" and recommends Bayesian intervals — [Robot Policy Evaluation Harness](https://shubhamrasal-robot-policy-eval.hf.space/); a practitioner guide suggests about 50 nominal trials plus about 20 trials at each perturbed condition, with trial order randomised/interleaved — [Claru guide](https://claru.ai/guides/how-to-evaluate-policy-performance)
- VLA-REPLICA (2026) defines protocols for both in-distribution and out-of-distribution settings on off-the-shelf hardware and reports consistent results across independently built setups — [arXiv 2605.20774](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.20774)
- Suture following on a low-cost arm (2026) evaluated 28 models across 32 configurations along three axes (dataset size, camera viewpoint, background variation) and reports a "stitch completion rate" (92% for the best model) alongside task success — [arXiv 2605.28736](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.28736)

### Inferences
- A protocol that matches both the literature norm and the team's budget: (1) pre-register success as "puck centre within d_goal cm of the line end AND puck centre never more than d_lat cm from the line centre at any step" with d_goal and d_lat fixed from the demonstration set the way Diffusion Policy fixes its IoU threshold from the demos (e.g. the worst demo's max deviation); (2) report fraction of path completed (progress along the line) and max/mean lateral deviation per trial as graded metrics, mirroring the lateral-deviation and distance metrics of line-following papers and ACT's per-subtask stages; (3) at least 20 trials per sheet and condition, with starting puck positions marked on an overlay so they can be reproduced, all conditions interleaved in one session; (4) report raw counts (k/n) with a binomial interval, and a short failure-category table. With a 25-40 s episode and resets, 20 trials is about 20-30 minutes of robot time, so 20 trials per sheet across several sheets is feasible.
- Lateral deviation can be measured from a top-down phone photo of the sheet at the end or from the wrist camera via the stored intrinsics plus a sheet homography; the LineMaster paper used a high-speed camera, which is unnecessary at ~1 cm/s.
- "Unseen" should be defined before training as held-out path geometries whose curvature and turn count lie inside the range seen in training (in-distribution generalisation), with a separate, clearly labelled harder sheet if an out-of-distribution test is wanted, following Kress-Gazit et al.'s instruction to state which it is.
- Twenty trials cannot separate 13/20 from 14/20, so comparisons between model variants should be large-effect-only or use more trials; for the course report a single well-documented 20-30 trial evaluation per sheet is defensible.

### Gaps
- No paper was found that evaluates imitation learning on a manipulator path-following/line-tracing task with a published lateral-deviation threshold; the closest analogues are mobile line followers (SAC-PID, LineMaster) and suture following (which reports completion rate without a deviation metric in the abstract).
- The LineMaster per-condition "50 runs" detail and the SAC-PID "20 trials per path" detail come from search excerpts, not the full texts.
- Kress-Gazit et al. do not prescribe a specific interval method (Clopper-Pearson vs Bayesian); their worked examples use a uniform-prior Beta posterior.

## Key question 5: Practical pitfalls reported with low-cost arms for imitation learning (timing jitter, image-action misalignment, auto-exposure, lighting changes, backlash/repeatability) and the mitigations used

### Takeaway
The best-documented pitfalls are observation-action timing mismatch (UMI shows a 30-point success drop without latency matching), lighting shifts (success collapsing from 0.70 to as low as 0.10 under colour shifts), slow or irregular recording loops, camera placement drift, and distribution shift between sessions; the standard mitigations are measuring and matching latency, fixing exposure/white balance, keeping camera pose and scene constant between recording and testing, action chunking with temporal ensembling, and diversifying demonstrations rather than merely adding more.

### Cited Findings
- UMI (2024): latency of each stream is measured separately (camera end-to-end via a rolling QR-code timestamp on a monitor; proprioception from robot timestamps or half the ICMP round-trip; gripper by cross-correlating commanded and measured sinusoids); RGB frames are down-sampled to the policy frequency, "often 10-20Hz", and proprioception is linearly interpolated to each image's capture timestamp; actions are sent "ahead of time to compensate for robots' execution latency" and outdated actions are discarded; ablation on dynamic tossing: success fell from 87.5% (105/120) to 57.5% (69/120) without latency matching and movement became "much more jittery due to the out-of-sync observations and executions" — [arXiv 2402.10329 (HTML v3)](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.10329v3)
- Diffusion Policy (2023): the policy "predicts robot commands at 10 Hz and these commands then linearly interpolated to 125 Hz"; receding-horizon control "helps address the latency gap caused by image processing, policy inference, and network delay" — [arXiv 2303.04137 (HTML v5)](https://arxiv.org/html/2303.04137v5)
- ACT (2023): behaviour cloning suffers "compounding errors, where errors from previous timesteps" push the robot out of distribution; action chunking plus temporal ensembling is the mitigation, "both action chunking and temporal ensembling" found necessary; the paper also notes a black table top "creates a low-contrast against many objects" as a perception difficulty — [ACT paper PDF](https://www.roboticsproceedings.org/rss19/p016.pdf); [ACT project page](https://tonyzhaozh.github.io/aloha/)
- LeRobot camera docs: `read()` "May block for a long time depending on the camera and SDK"; `async_read(timeout_ms)` returns the latest unconsumed frame; `read_latest(max_age_ms)` "may be stale" and raises if the frame is older than `max_age_ms`; manual `exposure`, `gain`, `white_balance` settings "disable the corresponding automatic exposure or white-balance mode" (RealSense only); camera identifiers "may change after rebooting your computer or re-plugging the camera" — [LeRobot cameras documentation](https://huggingface.co/docs/lerobot/cameras)
- LeRobot dataset timestamp check: frame intervals are compared with 1/fps using `tolerance_s` (default 1e-4) and an error is raised on deviation; a blog suggests raising it to 5e-3 as a stopgap and using `delta_timestamps` to compensate a consistent lag (e.g. image trailing state by 200 ms) (secondary, unverified source) — [CSDN blog on LeRobot tolerance_s](https://adg.csdn.net/697072a8437a6b40336a4972.html)
- Forum case (August 2026): recording loop at ~5.6 Hz against a 30 Hz target with two USB webcams; policy consistently missed to the right; advice was to check teleoperation speed versus rollout speed, camera transport/USB bandwidth, calibration and camera placement before collecting more data, and to evaluate covered versus held-out positions separately — [HF forum thread](https://discuss.huggingface.co/t/what-is-the-expected-success-rate-for-a-act-trained-model-using-50-episodes/178517)
- Lighting: a 2026 lighting study (RoboLight, Jin et al.) varies illumination colour, direction and intensity with a programmable LED "Light Cube" and 2,800 real episodes; the search excerpt of its overview reports that even a 50% shift in lighting colour or direction caused a significant drop in success, with one task falling from 0.70 to as low as 0.10 under colour shift, most failures attributed to object mislocalisation — [arXiv 2603.04249](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.04249); [alphaxiv overview](https://www.alphaxiv.org/overview/2603.04249)
- Auto-exposure: a CVPR 2024 paper states that poorly adjusted camera exposure "often leads to critical failure and performance degradation" of vision pipelines (search excerpt) — [Lee et al., CVPR 2024](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2024/papers/Lee_Learning_to_Control_Camera_Exposure_via_Reinforcement_Learning_CVPR_2024_paper.pdf); a camera-forum user resolved dark/noisy auto-ISO frames by manually fixing exposure time and ISO (search excerpt) — [Luxonis forum](https://discuss.luxonis.com/d/392/3)
- Session-to-session shift: Kress-Gazit et al. report a policy dropping to 0/8 after a lab reorganisation versus 13/20 before, and recommend evaluating all policies within one session so lighting and environment match — [arXiv 2409.09491](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.09491v1)
- Camera placement: an SO-100 school test-stand write-up notes that because the policy learns from pixels, a camera shifted by a few centimetres presents a different scene than the one it trained on (search excerpt) — [van-i/so100-imitation-learning-stand](https://huggingface.co/van-i/so100-imitation-learning-stand)
- Niryo-specific: the Vision Set stream over Wi-Fi "may overload it, generating some latency between the commands and the robot reaction"; use Ethernet — [Warnings & Typical issues](https://docs.niryo.com/product/vision-set/source/warnings_typical_issues.html); Ned2 relative encoders require calibration after every reboot and give "bad encoder readings" near end-stops — [ned2.mdx](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/NiryoRobotics/lerobot/main/docs/source/ned2.mdx); Niryo x HF uses link-local IPs for low latency — same source
- Backlash/repeatability: an industrial-robot study found that "backlash error strongly affects the repeatability of the industrial robot" (search excerpt) — [Cambridge Robotica article](https://www.cambridge.org/core/product/identifier/S0263574711001287/type/journal_article); a 2026 IEEE Access paper covers backlash identification and compensation for repeatability — [IEEE Access via DOAJ](https://doaj.org/article/03e81c3f92ff413db48e030934c6c10d); Ned2 is specified at +/- 0.5 mm repeatability (ISO 9283) — [robots.ros.org/ned2](https://robots.ros.org/ned2/)
- Low-cost arm failure modes (2026): "repetition loops and grasp instability" are common execution failures across policies on the SO-101, and the IL baseline shows higher state-mismatch rates — [arXiv 2606.08881](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.08881); depth error dominated failures in suture following on an open-source arm — [arXiv 2605.28736](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.28736)

### Inferences
- The team's blocking interface converts the hardest pitfall (observation-action timing mismatch) into a non-issue if, and only if, each image is captured after the previous move has returned and before the next is sent; the recorded pair (image_t, step_t) is then exactly aligned with no interpolation. The flip side is that any pipelining attempt (reading the camera while a move executes) is impossible on one connection and would reintroduce misalignment.
- Compounding error is the main remaining risk: at 1 cm per step a 25 cm path is 25-40 sequential decisions, each conditioned on the result of the last. Mitigations transferable from the literature: record demonstrations with deliberate small lateral perturbations and corrections (so the policy sees recovery), predict short chunks of 2-3 steps, and keep the action space to a small discrete set of displacements so a wrong step is bounded.
- Lighting is the second risk: the RoboLight and Kress-Gazit numbers imply the final unseen-sheet test should be run in the same room, same lamps, same time of day as recording, with any auto-exposure behaviour of the wrist camera checked (the pyniryo client exposes no exposure control, so the mitigation is constant lighting plus brightness/contrast augmentation during training rather than fixed exposure).
- Puck/line colours: the red puck on a black line on white paper is high contrast; the ACT note about low-contrast black table tops suggests keeping the table around the sheet visually distinct from the line (e.g. not a black mat).
- Ned2's 0.5 mm repeatability and relative-encoder calibration are not a precision problem at the 1 cm step scale, but calibration after every reboot must be in the session checklist, and the "sheet 5 cm in front of the base, line pointing away" placement (from the workspace memory note) must be reproduced with markings so the test matches the demonstrations.

### Gaps
- No source quantifies backlash or repeatability effects for the Ned2 specifically in a learning context; the backlash citations are for industrial arms and SO-100/Feetech servos.
- The RoboLight success-drop numbers (0.70 to 0.10, 50% shift) come from a search excerpt of an overview page, not from the paper text fetched (abstract only); confirm in the full paper before quoting precise values.
- The CSDN note on `tolerance_s` and `delta_timestamps` is a secondary source; the official LeRobot behaviour should be checked in the dataset code if the team adopts LeRobot's dataset format.
- Whether the Niryo wrist camera applies auto-exposure/auto-white-balance, and whether it can be disabled on the robot side (e.g. via v4l2 over SSH), was not found in any source.
