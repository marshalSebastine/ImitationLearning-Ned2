  at 169.254.200.200 (Wi-Fi stays free for the internet). Only ONE program can be connected at a time; a second connection
  hangs forever. Every move command blocks until done; a 1 cm tool step costs about 0.77 s during a push (measured 25 Oct),

- Pusher: the gripper is removed; Niryo's magnet block is snapped onto the bare rod; its round magnet tip (1.3 cm wide, 2.6 cm
  below the rod's end) pushes the puck 5 mm above the paper. Puck: 2.4 cm wide, 2.9 cm tall, 9 g.
- Camera: Niryo Vision Set (640x480 JPEG through the robot, about 9 new pictures per second measured; Niryo's calibration
  says a 64-degree lens). It sits in Niryo's black holder, fixed by two silver hex screws, one through each of the holder's
  two ears, into the aluminium brace on the WRIST link, beside the rod's top (photo
  `03_data/raw/camera_check/2026-10-26_wrist_camera_holder_two_ear_screws.jpg`). It does not turn with the hand (joint 6),
  it tilts with the wrist (joint 5), and with the rod vertical it always looks straight away from the base. Measured window:
  about 8.5 cm wide at the puck, 10 cm at the top, from the puck's centre to about 7 cm ahead; the puck sits at the bottom of
  every picture. Consequence, calculated from that window and from tracing the six sheet photos (not yet tested on the
  robot): the line stays in view only while the path heads within about 45 degrees of "away from the base", so hairpins and
  loops (sheets 03 to 06) cannot be followed by sight with this mount. The lens has a MANUAL focus ring, set by eye on
  25 Oct; do not touch it except for the one planned refocus that checklist step 4's no-touch sweep may call for, done off
  the robot as checklist step 10 describes.
- Sheet 01 (straight line) lies 5 cm in front of the base with the line pointing away, taped; taught by touching six points
  (`02_code/teach_positions.py`): line from x = 0.156 m to 0.398 m, y about -0.02 m. The start pose is computed in
  `02_code/config.py`. The robot reaches start and goal within 1 mm. Scripted straight pushes (`02_code/push_straight.py`)
  reach the goal with the puck in view in every picture; these were hardware checks, not training data.
- Lessons that cost time: never push on the arm while the motors hold it (joint 4 overloaded once, error 32, fixed by a
  restart); never do a manual calibration (zero was off by degrees; only the automatic one after a restart); motors off
  (FreeMotion) before anyone touches the arm; close any camera viewer before running another script (one connection);
  pyniryo's own image decoder fails with numpy 2, so every script decodes the JPEG with cv2.imdecode.

## 3. Decisions taken

1. Imitation learning only; the policy sees only the camera; relative 1 cm steps plus a stop flag as the action.
2. Pusher: the magnet block on the rod (allowed by the professor). Camera: keep the Vision Set.
3. Camera mount. A printed collar on the rod with the camera panned by joint 6 was proposed on 26 Oct and WITHDRAWN the
   same evening after a five-reviewer design review against Niryo's meshes: the forearm fork's two cheeks hang about 5 mm
   below the rod's top on both sides, so a camera body beside the rod cannot pan. The daytime note "no new mount, use the
   joints" in the meeting note was overtaken by the same review: the joints alone reach at most 67 to 79 percent of a hard
   sheet. Default now: a tall printed wrist bracket (option F: camera about 5 cm ahead of the rod axis and 6 cm above the
   wrist axis, looking nearly straight down; calculated to see 89 to 98 percent of the hard sheets with no software change).
   F is calculated only: the design review (risk M17) finds its camera body touching the forearm at tip x = 0.14 m, so the
   mock-up in checklist step 5 must measure that gap or the sheet starts 2 cm further out; F may also need one refocus to
   about 12 cm (risk M15). It is reached by the staged path in `checklist_camera_mount.md`: Maker Space questions,
   measurements with calipers, a mock-up with the camera held or clamped, the decision, the design from measured numbers
   only, a 1:1 paper test, and the print only as a copy of a geometry photographed working. Two pictures of the collar are
   kept under `figures/` with WITHDRAWN in their names; do not use them.
4. Demonstrations: keyboard stepping is the default per the SOTA report (training pictures equal test pictures); the phase-2
   guide's step 4 trial (three pushes each way) still decides, and its result goes into a method decision record.
5. Grading rule drives everything: finish first, speed second.
