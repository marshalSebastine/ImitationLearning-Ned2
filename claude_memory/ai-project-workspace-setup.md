Set-up on the OTH desk as of 25.10.26 (see [[user-ai-project-team]]):

- Sheet: the straight-line sheet (path_01) lies in front of the robot with the black line pointing straight away from the base and centred. By tape measure: 5 cm from the front of the base to the paper's near edge, the paper's far edge 2 cm from the table's far edge, the base 7 cm from the table's side edge. Taped down. Overhead photo: `06_deliverables/team_m_project6_documentation/figures/setup_2026-10-25_overhead.jpg`.
- In robot coordinates (taught by touching six points, `02_code/teach_positions.py`): line from x = 0.156 m to 0.398 m, y about -0.02 m; sheet 28 by 19 cm; table plane in `config.py`.
- Pusher (since 25.10.26 afternoon): the gripper is removed; the Niryo magnet block is snapped onto the bare metal rod and its round magnet tip (1.3 cm wide, 2.6 cm below the rod's end) pushes the puck. Start pose = tip 1.85 cm behind the puck centre, 5 mm above the paper, rod pointing straight down (`config.start_pose()`, extension handled in `PUSHER_PARAMETERS`).
- Puck: red wooden cylinder, 2.4 cm across, 2.9 cm tall, 9 g (taller than wide, so it is pushed low).
- Camera (Niryo Vision Set): in its ORIGINAL bracket, screwed on; nothing is taped on the robot (tape mounts on the wrist fork and on the rod were tried on 25.10 and undone). Where it sits, from evidence: joint 6 does not move it (four pictures with only joint 6 turned are identical), and all 37 push pictures of 25.10 show the same view while the forearm tilted about 40 degrees, so the camera moves with the WRIST (joint 5, the rod's lean), not with the forearm. Confirmed on 26.10.26 from Niryo's own set-up guide: the holder screws into the wrist's aluminium brace beside the joint-6 motor. Niryo's calibration file gives a 64-degree lens (not the 104 quoted elsewhere). Niryo's STEP files of the whole Ned2 and of the electromagnet are saved under `06_deliverables/research_notes/Camera mount options/niryo_files/`. My 25.10 note "on the forearm, tilt changes 55 degrees" was computed, never observed, and is wrong.
- What the camera sees with the rod straight down (measured from the push pictures): a window about 8.5 cm wide at the puck and 10 cm at the top, from the puck's centre to about 7 cm ahead of it; the puck sits at the bottom of every picture; the window always points straight away from the base (radially), whatever way the line goes, because a vertical rod forces the forearm roll (joint 4) to zero. The line ahead stays visible only while the travel direction is within about 45 degrees of "away from the base"; the puck itself up to about 60 degrees. Hairpins and loops (sheets 03 to 06) cannot be followed by sight with this mount. No joint turns 360 degrees (forearm roll +-120, hand +-145). Only near x = 0.18 to 0.22 m is the forearm vertical, where joint 4 can turn the camera sideways at little rod tilt (model, untested). Leaning the rod makes the window smaller, not larger (model). The option study of 26.10.26 first ranked a printed collar on the rod (panned by joint 6), but the design review of the same evening (`06_deliverables/research_notes/Camera mount options/design_review_one_print.md`) refuted it: the forearm fork's cheeks hang about 5 mm below the rod's top on both sides, so a camera body beside the rod cannot pan. Current ranking: tall printed wrist bracket F (89 to 98 percent of the hard sheets; needs a body-level clearance check at tip x below 0.16 m) > forearm roll in the band (67 to 79) > as now (35 to 55). The team gets ONE print: measure with calipers, prototype with a hose clamp, test the software with the stock camera, and print only a geometry photographed working. The professor allows a printed camera mount (Maker Space prints it) and the magnet block as pusher.
- Picture rate about 9 new pictures per second; the recorder must wait for a changed JPEG after each move (a stale repeated frame was found in run 1419 step 23).
- A fixed metal cable-hole lid sits on the table beyond the sheet's far edge, left side; it appears at the top of the picture at the end of the line and must stay out of the training view where possible.
- Lessons: never push on the arm while the motors hold it (joint 4 overload); never use manual calibration (zero off by degrees), automatic calibration after a restart fixes it; never touch the camera or its focus ring.

**Why:** Emranul asked to remember the set-up; recordings only transfer if the exam set-up is rebuilt the same way, and the beginner guides must describe this hardware exactly. The camera facts decide what paths the robot can follow and were got wrong once at a cost of hours ([[feedback-test-before-hardware-changes]]).

**How to apply:** when the sheet is moved or the exam layout differs, re-run `teach_positions.py` (six touches, five minutes) rather than measuring by hand; keep the line pointing away from the robot because of the camera; before any claim about the camera, check it against the pictures on disk or a two-minute robot test.
